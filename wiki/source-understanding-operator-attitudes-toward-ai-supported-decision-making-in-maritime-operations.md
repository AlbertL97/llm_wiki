# Understanding Operator Attitudes Toward AI-Supported Decision Making in Maritime Operations

## Findings

This study explored maritime professionals' perspectives on AI-supported decision-making systems in collision-avoidance scenarios within Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS). The research found that while participants generally hold positive attitudes towards maritime technology and exhibit stable trust in automation, they also expressed concerns about AI reliability, over-reliance, and potential loss of human expertise.

Key insights include:

*   **Positive Disposition:** A generally favorable view of maritime technology was noted among stakeholders.
*   **Trust and Reliance:** Trust in automation was consistent across various scenarios, but the focus for future systems should be on enabling "calibrated reliance" rather than simply increasing trust or automation levels.
*   **Explanation Quality:** The perceived quality of AI explanations was influenced by the specific scenario, indicating a need for nuanced and context-aware explanations.
*   **Valued Support:** Participants appreciated AI's role in aiding decision-making, improving situation awareness, and fostering confidence.
*   **Concerns:** Significant concerns were raised about the reliability of AI, the risk of users becoming overly dependent on these systems, and the potential erosion of essential human expertise.

## Recommendations

The paper suggests that the design of maritime AI systems should emphasize transparency, reliability, and operational relevance. It advocates for the continued involvement of domain experts throughout the development and implementation process to ensure these systems effectively support operators without undermining human capabilities.
