# Efficient Test-Time Adaptation through Human-AI Interaction

## Overview

This paper proposes Test-Time Adaptation through Human-AI Interaction (TAHI), a novel approach to personalize AI agents to meet the specific and often implicit criteria of individual users. It addresses the challenge that AI agents, trained on broad datasets, often fail to meet the precise standards of professionals. TAHI leverages iterative human-AI interaction data to refine agent behavior, effectively capturing and integrating user-specific expertise and evaluation criteria.

## Key Findings

*   **Personalization through Interaction:** TAHI integrates cross-session human-agent interaction data to adapt AI agents to individual users' unique needs and expertise, which are often difficult to articulate explicitly.
*   **Improved Task Success:** The adapted agents demonstrated significant improvements in task success rates, ranging from 4.5% to 20.9% across 30 individuals in domains like writing and visual creation, after adaptation on a limited number of tasks.
*   **Evolving Rubric Module:** An "evolving rubric module" was developed as part of TAHI. This module acts as a scalable annotation tool, creating personalized evaluation rubrics that are more effective than those generated by language models or humans alone, catching 16.0% to 22.3% more failures.
*   **Generalization of Improvements:** Despite personalization, the performance enhancements achieved by the adapted agents showed a degree of generalization, improving success rates for other users by up to 8.8%.

## Methodology

The TAHI method incorporates user interaction data into the agent's context and weights. It also features an "evolving rubric module" that learns and refines evaluation criteria over time based on user feedback and task outcomes.

## Impact

This work highlights the value of continuous human-AI collaboration for achieving high-performance, personalized AI systems. It offers a practical framework for tailoring AI to individual expertise and developing more accurate and scalable methods for evaluating AI performance in complex, open-ended tasks.
