# Designing Social Robots for Social-Cognition Training with Autistic Adults

## Overview

This research addresses a gap in the literature concerning social robots for autism intervention by focusing on the needs and design preferences of autistic adults. Previous efforts have predominantly targeted children and have often excluded the perspectives of autistic individuals themselves. This study employs a co-design approach to understand how social robots can be effectively developed to support social-cognition training for adults on the autism spectrum.

## Key Findings

*   **Focus on Adult Needs:** The study highlights that current social robot interventions often fail to meet the specific social-cognitive challenges faced by autistic adults, such as navigating complex social cues, managing conversation flow, and interpreting emotions.
*   **Co-design Process:** An online focus group and co-design session with five autistic adults were conducted to gather insights into the desired functionalities, interaction styles, and utility of a social robot for social-cognition training.
*   **Seven Core Design Requirements:** The analysis revealed seven crucial themes for designing effective social robots:
    *   **Scaffold, Not Substitute:** The robot should act as a support mechanism rather than a replacement for human interaction or real-world experiences.
    *   **Authenticity Over Comfort:** The design should prioritize genuine social simulation over merely creating a comfortable, non-challenging environment.
    *   **Personalized and User-Controlled Feedback:** Feedback provided by the robot needs to be tailored to the individual user and controllable by them.
    *   **Accommodate Emotional Self-Awareness Gaps:** The robot should be designed to assist users in understanding and managing their emotional states.
    *   **Respect Privacy and Contextual Boundaries:** The robot's operation must adhere to strict privacy protocols and be sensitive to social contexts.
    *   **Support Real-World Rehearsal:** The robot should facilitate practice for navigating actual social situations encountered in everyday life.
    *   **Configurable Identity, Form, and Expression:** Users should have the ability to customize aspects of the robot's appearance and behavior.
*   **Envisioned Role of the Robot:** Autistic adults view the social robot not as a companion or direct social assistant, but as a private, adaptable rehearsal tool intended to foster greater independence over time.
