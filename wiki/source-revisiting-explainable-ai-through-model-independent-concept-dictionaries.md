# Revisiting Explainable AI through Model-Independent Concept Dictionaries

**Last updated:** 2026-10-08

## Overview

This paper introduces DictXAI, a novel approach to Explainable AI (XAI) designed to overcome the limitations of existing methods. Traditional XAI techniques often rely on interpretable input features or complex, architecture-specific internal representations, which can hinder their practical application and consistency across different AI models. DictXAI addresses this by defining interpretable concepts directly within the input data space using a 'dictionary' of predefined elements.

## Key Findings

*   **Novelty of DictXAI:** The core innovation is the use of a dictionary of interpretable elements to represent concepts directly in the input domain, making explanations more transparent and less dependent on internal model specifics.
*   **Methodology:** DictXAI works by first computing a sparse code of the input data and then attributing the AI model's prediction to the relevant dictionary elements identified in this code.
*   **Actionability and Transparency:** The explanations generated by DictXAI are highlighted as being highly actionable. The method can pinpoint specific data artifact patterns that lead to AI malfunctions, such as Clever Hans effects (where an AI relies on spurious correlations).
*   **Human-AI Alignment:** DictXAI has been shown to improve alignment between human understanding and AI decision-making, particularly demonstrated in complex tasks involving biomedical signals.
*   **Versatility and Agnosticism:** The method is architecture-agnostic and can operate with various types of dictionaries, including learned image bases, analytically defined waveforms (e.g., for ECG), and experimentally derived elements.
*   **Superiority over Existing Methods:** The authors claim that DictXAI provides more interpretable, actionable, and universally applicable insights compared to classical XAI techniques and other concept-based approaches.

## Applications

*   **Medical AI:** Particularly effective for explaining AI decisions on intricate biomedical signals.
*   **Identifying AI Flaws:** Useful for diagnosing and understanding why an AI might make incorrect predictions, especially when relying on unintended data features.
