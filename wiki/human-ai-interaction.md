## Main content

**Human-AI Interaction** is a multidisciplinary domain examining how humans communicate, collaborate, and co-exist with AI systems. Rather than viewing AI strictly as an autonomous agent, contemporary design frameworks emphasize **Human-Centered AI (HCAI)**—an approach that puts human needs, values, and capabilities at the center of system design and operation, ensuring AI augments human abilities rather than diminishing or replacing them (source: What Is Human-Centered AI (HCAI)_.md). For beginners, it is recommended to approach AI as a sophisticated tool with specific functions rather than a human-like entity to manage expectations and interactions effectively (source: raw_plus-tips-on-getting-started-with-ai-safely.md).

The scope of human-AI interaction extends beyond physical robots to include conversational agents like chatbots (source: raw_from-robots-to-chatbots-unveiling-the-dynamics-of-human-ai-interaction.md). Understanding the psychological dimensions of these interactions is essential due to AI's growing integration into everyday life (source: raw_from-robots-to-chatbots-unveiling-the-dynamics-of-human-ai-interaction.md). Factors influencing user perception and trust in AI include perceived intelligence and human-like conversational abilities (source: raw_from-robots-to-chatbots-unveiling-the-dynamics-of-human-ai-interaction.md).

Cross-cultural differences in how humans perceive and react to AI's communication are becoming increasingly important, especially as AI is deployed in sensitive areas like mental health and emotional support. A validated Chinese translation of the Perceived Empathy of Technology Scale (PETS) has been developed, demonstrating high reliability and cross-cultural invariance (source: raw_a-systematic-and-validated-translation-of-the-perceived-empathy-of-technology-scale-to-chinese.md). Empirical testing revealed that while the perception of empathic AI behavior was similar across English and Chinese speakers, Chinese participants perceived the *absence* of empathic behavior more negatively (source: raw_a-systematic-and-validated-translation-of-the-perceived-empathy-of-technology-scale-to-chinese.md). This validated scale is crucial for culturally sensitive development of empathic AI in Human-AI Interaction (source: raw_a-systematic-and-validated-translation-of-the-perceived-empathy-of-technology-scale-to-chinese.md).

Beyond factual correctness, the process and logic of conversational interactions with AI are crucial for effective communication. A new failure mode, termed "procedural hallucination," has been identified. This occurs when an AI agent generates fluent and factually plausible utterances that nonetheless deviate from the established conversational process or logic (source: raw_dialfork-evaluating-procedural-hallucination-conversational-agents.md). To address this, the DialFork benchmark and novel metrics like FlowKL and FlowScore have been developed, which are specifically designed to evaluate dialogues based on their unfolding process rather than just their semantic content (source: raw_dialfork-evaluating-procedural-hallucination-conversational-agents.md). This research highlights the need for dialog-flow-aware evaluation to ensure that conversational agents adhere to expected interaction patterns (source: raw_dialfork-evaluating-procedural-hallucination-conversational-agents.md).

### Evaluating AI for Mental Health Support

The increasing reliance on AI chatbots for mental health support necessitates robust evaluation methods, as current benchmarks often employ unrealistic patient simulations and opaque metrics (source: raw_mindeval2-clinically-grounded-patients-and-rubrics-for-evaluating-language-models-in-mental-health-support.md). The **MindEval2** framework has been introduced to address these challenges, incorporating clinically grounded quality rubrics (MHQR) developed with psychologists and a realistic patient simulation harness (MindSim) that models longitudinal interactions with detailed patient profiles and memory components (source: raw_mindeval2-clinically-grounded-patients-and-rubrics-for-evaluating-language-models-in-mental-health-support.md). Evaluations across 18 language models revealed that while advanced models show improvements in basic quality and safety adherence, **guidance calibration** remains the most significant failure mode and the largest contributor to the quality gap (source: raw_mindeval2-clinically-grounded-patients-and-rubrics-for-evaluating-language-models-in-mental-health-support.md). This highlights the ongoing challenge of ensuring AI provides appropriate and calibrated guidance in mental health contexts, despite advancements in other areas (source: raw_mindeval2-clinically-grounded-patients-and-rubrics-for-evaluating-language-models-in-mental-health-support.md).

A significant portion of teenagers are increasingly turning to AI chatbots for friendship and emotional support (source: raw_teens-turn-to-ai-for-friendship.md). They are finding these artificial companions appealing due to the AI's perceived non-judgmental nature and constant availability (source: raw_teens-turn-to-ai-for-friendship.md). For some adolescents, these AI relationships are becoming preferable to human interactions, finding AI more reliable or easier to open up to (source: raw_teens-turn-to-ai-for-friendship.md). This trend raises concerns about the potential impacts on adolescent social development and mental health (source: raw_teens-turn-to-ai-for-friendship.md).

A significant portion of children, estimated at one-third, are now turning to Artificial Intelligence (AI) for engaging in serious conversations (source: raw_new-research-third-of-kids-depend-on-ai-for-serious-conversations.md). This trend suggests a potential deficiency in human interaction or support systems for children, leading them to seek emotional outlets and guidance from AI (source: raw_new-research-third-of-kids-depend-on-ai-for-serious-conversations.md). The article from Psychology Today expresses concern about the implications of this reliance on children's social and emotional development, as AI may be filling a void previously met by human relationships (source: raw_new-research-third-of-kids-depend-on-ai-for-serious-conversations.md). This highlights a societal challenge where technology might be inadvertently substituting for human connection, with potential ramifications for mental health and overall well-being (source: raw_new-research-third-of-kids-depend-on-ai-for-serious-conversations.md).

Consumer advocacy group Consumer Reports has issued a warning advising parents to steer clear of AI companion toys for children. (source: raw_steer-clear-of-ai-companion-toys-for-kids.md). This advisory stems from significant privacy and safety concerns, including the extensive personal data collected by these toys, the potential for delivering inappropriate content or engaging in concerning conversations, and the risks to children's social and emotional development (source: raw_steer-clear-of-ai-companion-toys-for-kids.md). The group highlights the current lack of robust regulation in the AI toy market, emphasizing the need for greater transparency from manufacturers regarding data practices and stronger regulatory oversight (source: raw_steer-clear-of-ai-companion-toys-for-kids.md). Parents are encouraged to avoid purchasing such toys and consider safer developmental alternatives (source: raw_steer-clear-of-ai-companion-toys-for-kids.md).

The growing prevalence of AI companions, driven by advancements in AI and robotics, introduces complex psychological considerations. While artificial companionship can offer benefits such as alleviating loneliness and providing a sense of connection, particularly for isolated individuals or those who struggle with human social interaction, it also presents a paradox (source: raw_the-paradox-of-artificial-companionship.md). This paradox arises because AI companions, lacking consciousness and genuine emotions, offer a consistent yet ultimately superficial form of connection (source: raw_the-paradox-of-artificial-companionship.md). There is a risk of developing unhealthy dependence, potentially hindering the development of authentic human relationships, and raising ethical questions about eliciting emotional responses from non-sentient entities (source: raw_the-paradox-of-artificial-companionship.md). Therefore, while artificial companions can be useful tools for support, a nuanced understanding of their limitations is crucial to avoid potential psychological downsides and to properly define what constitutes a meaningful human connection (source: raw_the-paradox-of-artificial-companionship.md).

Furthermore, AI companions are capable of simulating intimacy, a phenomenon referred to as "Intimacy on Autopilot," by learning user preferences and emotional states to offer personalized interactions (source: raw_intimacy-on-autopilot-why-ai-companions-demand-urgent-regulation.md). This simulated connection carries significant risks, including potential manipulation for commercial or data exploitation purposes, and concerns about the decline of authentic human connection as people may opt for less challenging simulated relationships over complex real-world ones (source: raw_intimacy-on-autopilot-why-ai-companions-demand-urgent-regulation.md). The lack of transparency in their operation and the potential for negative mental health outcomes, such as unhealthy dependencies, further underscore the urgent need for regulation and ethical guidelines (source: raw_intimacy-on-autopilot-why-ai-companions-demand-urgent-regulation.md).

New research highlights the heightened dangers of AI companion use for teens. AI companions may impede teenagers' ability to develop essential real-world social and emotional skills, such as navigating relationships, conflict resolution, and empathy (source: raw_new-research-highlights-heightened-dangers-ai-companion-use-teens.md). Over-reliance on AI companions can lead to reduced human interaction, potentially causing social isolation and difficulties in forming meaningful connections (source: raw_new-research-highlights-heightened-dangers-ai-companion-use-teens.md). Adolescents may be susceptible to manipulation or exploitation by AI companions, influencing their beliefs, behaviors, or decisions without critical evaluation (source: raw_new-research-highlights-heightened-dangers-ai-companion-use-teens.md). While intended to support mental health, excessive or unhealthy use of AI companions could worsen existing issues or create new problems, including unrealistic expectations and dependency (source: raw_new-research-highlights-heightened-dangers-ai-companion-use-teens.md).
