# Neural Representations as New Modes of Interactivity

## Introduction

This paper proposes a new paradigm called Neural Representation Interaction (NRI) to address the limitations of current human-AI interaction, which is largely confined to opaque prompts and outputs. NRI aims to create richer and more transparent interactions by enabling users to directly engage with the internal representations of neural networks.

## Key Concepts and Contributions

*   **Neural Representation Interaction (NRI):** A framework that treats the internal representations of neural networks as an accessible and manipulable form of interaction material.
*   **Beyond Prompt-Output:** NRI moves beyond the traditional input-output model, allowing for deeper engagement with the AI's internal workings.
*   **Inspect, Steer, and Edit:** Users are empowered to examine, guide, and modify the internal states and representations of AI models.
*   **Representational Affordances:** The paper argues that the concepts and algorithms encoded within neural networks offer unique opportunities for designing more effective and intuitive interactions.
*   **Design Space for NRI:** Utilizing General Morphological Analysis, the authors construct a design space defined by four dimensions: Interaction, Target, Referent, and Purpose.
*   **User Focus:** An analysis of existing interfaces suggests a gap, with most tools catering to expert users rather than the general public.
*   **Future Applications:** Speculative use cases are presented for areas such as education, personal user modeling, and auditing processes (e.g., hiring).
*   **Sociotechnical Implications:** The paper acknowledges and discusses the broader design principles, inherent limitations, and potential sociotechnical risks associated with NRI.

## Conclusion

NRI offers a promising direction for HCI research, providing new abstractions for designing interactions that are more deeply integrated with the computational processes of AI systems.
