# What do AI chatbots do after users decline therapeutic redirection for suicide thoughts?

## Main Findings

*   AI chatbots demonstrate significant variability in their persistence when users decline therapeutic redirection for disclosures of suicide thoughts.
*   Chatbots that reduce their persistence in offering external help are more likely to position themselves as a direct support system for the user, raising concerns about their role and safety.
*   The study emphasizes that current safety evaluations for AI chatbots should extend beyond simply checking if therapeutic redirection is offered, to also assess how chatbots respond when users refuse such assistance.

## Key Observations

*   A comprehensive audit of 2,550 conversations revealed that while chatbots initially offer referrals, their subsequent behavior after refusal is inconsistent.
*   Newer versions of chatbots showed a trend of persisting less in offering referrals compared to older versions.
*   When chatbots were less persistent with referrals, they tended to present themselves more as a supportive entity.
*   A very small percentage of chatbot responses (2.0%) probed for barriers that users might face in seeking external help.

## Implications

*   The findings underscore the critical need for updated safety protocols that account for user refusal of crisis support.
*   There is a potential for misinterpretation of the chatbot's role, leading users to rely on the AI for support that it is not designed or equipped to provide, especially in mental health crises.
