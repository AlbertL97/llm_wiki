# Affective Tools for Thought: Towards Shared Attention and Affective Reorienting in AI-Supported Thinking

## Overview

This paper introduces the concept of "Affective Tools for Thought" (TfTs), arguing that current AI tools neglect the constitutive role of affect in cognition. Drawing from enactive cognitive science, the authors propose that affect is not just a factor that influences the speed of thought, but actively reshapes its very trajectory. They identify two primary barriers in existing systems: the lack of **Shared Attention** (AI's directed care for the user's engagement) and **Affective Reorienting** (AI's ability to use emotional moments to pivot thinking). To address these, three design strategies are suggested: **Chain of Emotion X Chain of Thought**, **Affective Mirror**, and **Prompted Reorienting**. These ideas are substantiated by empirical findings from a study on a touch-aware conversational agent used for embodied craft learning.

## Key Findings & Contributions

*   **Affect is Constitutive of Cognition:** Challenges the view of affect as merely 'friction' or an optimization signal, proposing it actively shapes cognitive processes.
*   **Barriers to Affective AI:** Identifies the lack of shared attention and affective reorienting as critical limitations in current AI-supported thinking tools.
*   **Proposed Design Strategies:**
    *   **Chain of Emotion X Chain of Thought:** Integrates emotional states directly with reasoning processes.
    *   **Affective Mirror:** AI reflects and acknowledges user's affective states to build rapport and understanding.
    *   **Prompted Reorienting:** AI uses affective cues to guide users toward novel cognitive paths.
*   **Empirical Basis:** Strategies are informed by a study involving an embodied learning agent, providing practical grounding.
