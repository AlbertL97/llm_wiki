# HEIR: Harness Egocentric Intent for Human-Robot Interactions

## Overview

This paper introduces **HEIR (Harness Egocentric Intent for Human-Robot Interactions)**, a new resource designed to advance the field of human-robot interaction (HRI). HEIR consists of a dataset and a framework that integrates multimodal human cues—speech, gaze, and egocentric video—with robot observations and teleoperated actions. The resource is structured to capture complete interaction episodes, where individual human requests are decomposed into multiple subtasks for the robot to execute. This approach facilitates in-depth research into critical HRI aspects like understanding human intent, planning robot actions, and successfully completing tasks.

## Key Findings and Contributions

*   **Development of the HEIR Resource**: A comprehensive dataset and framework are introduced, pairing human multimodal inputs (speech, gaze, egocentric video) with robot states and actions.
*   **Focus on Intent Grounding and Task Execution**: The HEIR resource is specifically designed to enable the study of how robots can accurately interpret human intentions and plan/execute complex, long-horizon tasks that involve navigation and manipulation.
*   **Naturalistic Interaction Setting**: The data is collected in naturalistic household environments, making the findings more applicable to real-world robot assistance and collaboration scenarios.
*   **Demonstrated Utility**: The authors provide evidence of HEIR's value through extensive offline experiments that highlight the benefits of incorporating human-centric context into AI models, as well as through real-robot experiments that validate task completion capabilities.
*   **Facilitating Future Research**: HEIR serves as a valuable tool for researchers aiming to develop more user-centric and effective robot interaction systems.
