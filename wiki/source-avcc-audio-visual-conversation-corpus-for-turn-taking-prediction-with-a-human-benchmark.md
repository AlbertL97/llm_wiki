# AVCC: Audio-Visual Conversation Corpus for Turn-Taking Prediction with a Human Benchmark

## Introduction

This paper introduces the Audio-Visual Conversation Corpus (AVCC), a novel dataset and benchmark designed to improve next-speaker prediction (NSP) in multi-party conversations. Accurate turn-taking is essential for natural interactions with conversational agents and social robots. The AVCC corpus addresses the limitations of existing NSP datasets by using a single camera and microphone setup, mimicking a typical robotic sensor configuration.

## Key Findings and Contributions

*   **AVCC Corpus Creation:** A 31-hour audio-visual corpus of two- and three-person online conversations was collected using a single static camera and microphone.
*   **Next-Speaker Prediction (NSP) Benchmark:** The corpus has been adapted to serve as a public benchmark for NSP tasks.
*   **Human Performance Benchmark:** A crucial aspect of this work is the inclusion of a human reference benchmark, allowing for direct comparison of AI model performance against human capabilities in predicting conversational turns.
*   **Dataset Features:** The benchmark includes a speaker-disjoint test partition with 865 'hold' and 865 'shift' events, as well as word-level forced-aligned transcripts for the entire corpus.
*   **Model Evaluation:** The paper evaluates existing models, including an audio-only RoleVAP model and the multimodal MuVAP model, on this new benchmark.
*   **Open Data Release:** All labels, transcripts, human performance data, and scripts to reproduce the results are made publicly available.

## Significance

This research contributes to making AI and robots better conversational partners by enabling them to predict conversational turns more accurately and naturally. The inclusion of a human benchmark provides a vital standard for evaluating the progress of AI in understanding human interaction dynamics.
