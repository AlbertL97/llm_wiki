# Beyond Bilingual: Studying Quadrilingual Code-Switching with Conversational AI

Last updated: 2026-11-17

This research examines how multilingual individuals engage in code-switching when interacting with conversational AI, moving beyond studies focused on only two well-resourced languages. The study took place in Curaçao and Aruba, regions where Dutch, English, Spanish, and the low-resource Creole Papiamentu/o are commonly used. In a preregistered experiment, 42 university students participated in two five-minute conversations with a chatbot, one about university life and another about local food and culture.

## Key Findings

*   **Code-Switching Patterns:** Participants primarily switched languages between turns rather than within a single message.
*   **Language Dominance:** English was the most frequently used language in both conversational contexts.
*   **Topic-Dependent Switching:** Language switching frequency increased during the conversation about local food and culture, with a corresponding rise in the use of Papiamentu/o and Spanish.
*   **Comfort with AI:** Self-reported comfort levels for language switching with the chatbot were comparable to switching with a human interlocutor.
*   **AI Capabilities:** The findings underscore the need for conversational agents to effectively manage turn-by-turn language shifts and to provide robust support for low-resource languages.

## Methodology

*   **Participants:** 42 university students in Curaçao and Aruba, plus a comparison group of 20 international students.
*   **Task:** Two five-minute chatbot conversations on predefined topics.
*   **Data Collection:** Chat logs and post-conversation surveys assessing language background, AI usage, and switching comfort.

## Implications

For effective human-AI interaction, particularly in diverse linguistic environments, conversational AI systems must be designed to accommodate natural multilingual behavior. This includes adapting to context-dependent language choices and ensuring adequate support for less commonly resourced languages.
