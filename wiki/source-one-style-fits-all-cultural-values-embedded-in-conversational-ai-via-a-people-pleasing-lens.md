# One Style Fits All? Cultural Values Embedded in Conversational AI via a People-Pleasing Lens

This study explores how the interaction styles of conversational AI, specifically supportive versus challenging approaches, impact user experience and continuance intention across different cultures. It also investigates whether an individual's people-pleasing tendency (PPT) influences these effects. Researchers found that supportive AI interactions consistently resulted in higher levels of user trust, satisfaction, and a greater likelihood of continued use. While PPT did not moderate the overall impact of interaction style, cultural variations were observed in how participants perceived threat, with higher PPT correlating with greater perceived threat among Taiwanese users but not Korean users. The study emphasizes the importance of culturally sensitive design in conversational AI, as generalized interaction styles can activate distinct cultural social scripts.

## Key Findings

*   **Impact of Interaction Style:** Supportive chatbot interactions led to higher continuance intention, satisfaction, and trust.
*   **Moderation by PPT:** People-pleasing tendency did not moderate the effects of interaction style on user experience.
*   **Cultural Differences in Threat Perception:** Higher PPT was associated with greater baseline threat perception in Taiwanese participants but not Korean participants.
*   **Design Implications:** General-purpose LLM styles can differentially activate culturally situated social scripts, necessitating culturally inclusive AI design.

## Methodology

*   **Participants:** Taiwanese (N=49) and Korean (N=52) participants.
*   **Task:** Collaborative tourism-planning task.
*   **Focus:** Examining the interplay between AI interaction style, individual differences (PPT), and cultural context on user experience.
