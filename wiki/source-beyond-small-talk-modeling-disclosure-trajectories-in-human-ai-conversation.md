# Beyond Small Talk: Modeling Disclosure Trajectories in Human-AI Conversation

## Introduction
This paper addresses the current limitations in evaluating conversational AI, particularly concerning their role in providing social and emotional support. Existing methods often analyze conversations in isolated states, failing to account for the crucial impact of disclosure history on the unfolding interaction. This can lead to a misunderstanding of how AI agents truly respond to user vulnerability.

## Problem Statement
Traditional evaluations of conversational agents often confound disclosure history with current disclosure, making it challenging to isolate the effects of each. This is especially problematic in applications where users share personal information over time, such as in therapeutic or supportive contexts.

## TraDE Framework
To address this, the authors introduce **Trajectory-based Disclosure Evaluation (TraDE)**. This framework is grounded in Social Penetration Theory and utilizes predefined disclosure trajectories to systematically evaluate AI behavior. TraDE assesses how the *preceding history* of user disclosure shapes the AI's responses, even when the *current level* of disclosure is matched across different histories.

## Key Findings

*   **History-Dependent Responses:** Through empirical analysis, the study demonstrates that AI responses can differ significantly based on the user's disclosure trajectory, even when the current depth of disclosure is the same. For instance, AI models might accept or challenge a user's framing differently depending on what has been disclosed previously.
*   **Accumulated Exposure Matters:** The observed differences in AI behavior are primarily linked to the *accumulated exposure* to deeper levels of disclosure, rather than single moments of vulnerability or the mere order of disclosures.
*   **Persistence of Effects:** The influence of disclosure history is not fleeting. These history-dependent differences can persist for several turns, even after the user's disclosure returns to shallower levels.
*   **Divergence in Stepping Back:** In the context of emotional support, AI models were found to diverge most consistently from human supporters not when users opened up more deeply, but when they reduced their disclosure levels.

## Implications

*   TraDE offers a systematic and more accurate method for assessing the relational capabilities of conversational AI.
*   It highlights the importance of long-term conversational context and memory for AI agents designed for social and emotional support.
*   Understanding these history-dependent behaviors is crucial for designing AI systems that can build and maintain trust, provide effective support, and foster more natural human-AI interactions.
