# XAI2CSI: Interpreting CSI with eXplainable AI for Human Activity Recognition

This paper introduces **XAI2CSI**, a novel framework that utilizes **eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)** to analyze and improve the robustness of Deep Learning (DL) models for **Human Activity Recognition (HAR)** based on **Wi-Fi Channel State Information (CSI)**. The core problem addressed is the poor generalization of existing DL models, which often fail when faced with different users, environments, or device configurations due to the inherent context-sensitivity of wireless signal propagation.

XAI2CSI employs the SAGE method to quantify how temporal, spectral, and spatial CSI features contribute to the model's HAR decisions. Evaluations revealed that the DL model heavily relies on context-specific CSI patterns, leading to significant misinterpretations and reduced performance when deployment conditions shift. The framework aims to provide insights for developing more transparent and dependable Wi-Fi sensing systems.

## Key Findings:

*   DL models for CSI-based HAR struggle with generalization across different contexts (users, environments, devices).
*   This generalization failure is often due to an over-reliance on context-specific CSI patterns.
*   XAI2CSI, using SAGE, quantifies the contributions of different CSI aspects to model decisions.
*   The proposed framework helps identify why models fail and guides the development of more robust systems.
