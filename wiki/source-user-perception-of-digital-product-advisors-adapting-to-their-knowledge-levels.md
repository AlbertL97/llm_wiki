# User Perception of Digital Product Advisors Adapting to Their Knowledge Levels

This study investigates how digital product advisors, such as chatbots in e-commerce, can effectively adapt their communication strategies to users with varying levels of domain knowledge, specifically focusing on the presentation of complex product information.

## Key Findings

*   **Adaptation Benefits Novices:** For users with low domain knowledge (novices), presenting technical product information augmented with explanations (TE) or explanations and categories (TCE) was perceived as more helpful and led to higher perceived learning compared to presenting technical information alone (T) or with categories only (TC).
*   **Explanations are Crucial for Categories:** Novice users found the combined approach (TCE) to be the most appropriate in terms of information quantity, underscoring that explanations are essential for novices to understand and benefit from performance categories.
*   **Experts Unaffected:** Critically, users with high domain knowledge (experts) did not report significant differences in their experience across the different presentation conditions. This suggests that providing supplementary information beneficial to novices does not negatively impact the experience of experts.
*   **Design Guidelines:** Based on these findings, the paper proposes four design guidelines for inclusive text-based product advisors in technical domains:
    *   Use the combined technical information, categories, and explanations (TCE) approach by default.
    *   Maintain a single, inclusive interface for all users.
    *   Avoid presenting categories as standalone information without explanations.
    *   Support user agency and personalization based on the stated use case.
