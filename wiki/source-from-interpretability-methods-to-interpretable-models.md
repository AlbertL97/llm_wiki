# From Interpretability Methods to Interpretable Models

## Summary

This paper critiques the current direction of Explainable AI (XAI), arguing that the field has overemphasized the development and comparison of interpretability methods without adequately addressing the core goal: making AI models understandable to humans. The authors propose a paradigm shift towards focusing on the models themselves and empirically measuring their interpretability from the perspective of independent human evaluators. This is crucial for building trust and enabling certification of AI systems.

## Key Findings

*   **Maturity of XAI Methods:** The field possesses a robust set of tools for interpretability, including attribution, feature visualization, concept-based, and circuit-based methods.
*   **Focus on Methods vs. Models:** Current XAI research predominantly focuses on developing and comparing methods, neglecting the assessment of true model interpretability and progress.
*   **Human-Centric Interpretability:** The ultimate goal of XAI should be to ensure models are understandable by the humans who rely on them, not just by domain experts.
*   **Measurement is Key:** Human understanding of AI models must be directly measured, as it cannot be reliably inferred from the performance of interpretability methods alone.
*   **Proposed Agenda:** The paper calls for a model-centric XAI agenda that prioritizes evaluating models based on their actual human interpretability.
