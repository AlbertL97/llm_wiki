# When Should I Trust This? Designing and Evaluating Factuality Indicators for LLM-based Chatbots

This paper explores how users perceive and react to factuality indicators, such as confidence levels and provided sources, when interacting with Large Language Model (LLM)-based chatbots. Through interviews, surveys, and a large-scale experiment, the research reveals that while these indicators can influence initial trust and perceptions of accuracy, users often interpret confidence ambiguously and raise concerns about source reliability. The study found that the impact of these indicators diminished once users learned the factual correctness of the AI's response, and that medical queries consistently generated skepticism. The authors advocate for better design of AI interfaces to help users understand model confidence and effectively utilize source information, ultimately aiming to cultivate calibrated trust in AI-assisted information retrieval.

## Key Findings

*   **User Verification:** Users actively and selectively verify AI-generated information using both external and AI-provided evidence.
*   **Confidence Indicators:** These indicators affect trust and accuracy perceptions, but interpretations of AI confidence are varied and often unclear.
*   **Source Indicators:** Sources are vital for user trust, yet concerns exist regarding their reliability and the effort needed for verification.
*   **Effectiveness of Indicators:** Initial perceptions are swayed by confidence and source indicators, but these effects are less pronounced once response correctness is known.
*   **Domain-Specific Skepticism:** Medical queries tend to be met with skepticism regardless of the presence or nature of factuality indicators.
*   **Design Recommendations:** Improved interfaces are needed to enhance user interpretation of model confidence and integrate source information to foster calibrated trust.

## Methodology

*   **Qualitative Methods:** Interviews (N=8) explored user strategies.
*   **Quantitative Methods:** A survey (N=1,000) and a large experiment (N=5,151) were conducted.
*   **Experimental Design:** Investigated the impact of confidence and source indicators on user perceptions for gardening, mathematical, and medical queries.
