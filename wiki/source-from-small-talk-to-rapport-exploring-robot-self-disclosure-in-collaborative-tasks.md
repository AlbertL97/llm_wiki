# From Small Talk to Rapport: Exploring Robot Self-Disclosure in Collaborative Tasks

This paper investigates how robot self-disclosure in small talk affects human-robot collaboration. Researchers developed an LLM-driven industrial manipulator with low and high self-disclosure strategies. A user study with 50 participants revealed that a **low-disclosure** strategy unexpectedly led to participants disclosing more and reporting stronger teaming and coordination with the robot. This effect was amplified in users with prior robot teaming experience. The findings suggest that increasing robot self-disclosure does not automatically enhance rapport or social connection, and other factors like user experience are critical.

## Key Findings

*   Lower robot self-disclosure led to increased participant self-disclosure.
*   Participants in the low-disclosure condition reported better teaming and coordination.
*   Prior HRI experience moderated the effect, with experienced users benefiting more from low-disclosure robots.
*   Increased robot self-disclosure may not be the sole or optimal path to building rapport or social connection.
