# Exploring fear in human-robot interaction: a scoping review of older adults’ experiences with social robots

This scoping review examines the emotion of fear within the context of human-robot interaction, specifically focusing on the experiences of older adults with social robots. It synthesizes existing literature to identify the various reasons behind these fears, such as concerns about robot capabilities, potential for harm, and the influence of media. The review underscores the importance of understanding and addressing these fears to facilitate the acceptance and effective use of social robots among the elderly, while also pointing to a need for further research in this area.

## Key Findings

*   **Prevalence of Fear:** Fear is a significant emotion experienced by older adults when interacting with social robots.
*   **Drivers of Fear:** Common sources of fear include concerns about robot malfunctions, physical harm, autonomy, privacy, and the impact of negative cultural narratives about robots.
*   **Barrier to Adoption:** Fear acts as a considerable barrier to the adoption and utilization of social robots by older adults.
*   **Research Gaps:** There is an identified need for more research to explore the nuances of fear in this population and develop effective mitigation strategies.
