# AnimalFarm: Auditing the Generation and Detection of Hateful Images Featuring Anthropomorphic Characters

This paper addresses the critical issue of hateful imagery generated by AI, specifically focusing on the use of anthropomorphic non-human characters. Researchers highlight that these characters, common in propaganda, are increasingly utilized in AI-generated content to spread hate. The study reveals a significant problem: current AI content detectors perform poorly when the primary subjects of hateful images are non-human, often failing to flag them as harmful.

## Key Findings:

*   **Prevalence of Hateful Content:** A large majority (95.9%) of images generated using anthropomorphic characters in the benchmark dataset contained hateful content.
*   **Detection Failures:** Existing detectors, including advanced models, struggle to identify hateful images when non-human characters are the main subjects. The best-performing detector only achieved a 73.9% identification rate, with others performing much worse.
*   **Root Cause:** Analysis indicates that detectors are biased towards recognizing hate speech when directed at human subjects, leading to misclassification of content involving non-human characters.
*   **Effective Mitigation Strategies:** The research proposes and validates two successful methods to address this gap:
    *   A training-free pipeline that converts non-human characters to human representations before classification, achieving an F1 score of 0.962.
    *   A detector specifically fine-tuned for anthropomorphic hateful content, reaching a high F1 score of 0.979.
*   **Safety Gaps:** The findings underscore significant vulnerabilities in both AI image generation safety protocols and the robustness of hateful-content detection systems.
