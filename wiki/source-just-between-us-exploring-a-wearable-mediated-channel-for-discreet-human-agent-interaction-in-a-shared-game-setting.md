# Just Between Us? Exploring a Wearable-Mediated Channel for Discreet Human-Agent Interaction in a Shared Game Setting

This paper investigates a novel approach to facilitate private communication between a human and an AI agent within a shared social context, specifically a game setting. The researchers developed and tested a wearable-mediated channel that combines in-ear audio messages with distinct head movements (rightward tilts) to enable discreet information exchange. 

## Key Findings:

*   **Discreet Communication Channel:** The study explored the use of a wearable device for private human-agent interaction, aiming to overcome limitations of public speech and gestures.
*   **Experimental Design:** A Wizard-of-Oz setup was employed where 20 pairs of participants played a card game. One participant acted as the 'wearer' of the device, interacting discreetly with a screen-based agent, while the other was a 'non-wearer'.
*   **Effectiveness:** The channel successfully facilitated information exchange for strategic decision-making (opponent selection) in the game, demonstrating its functional feasibility.
*   **User Experience (Wearer):** Participants using the channel reported low stress levels and found the system easy to use immediately after gameplay.
*   **User Experience (Non-Wearer):** Post-game interviews revealed that non-wearers had mixed perceptions regarding their awareness of the private interaction and differing views on the fairness of the gameplay.
*   **Design Implications:** The research underscores the potential of wearable technology for private human-agent communication and points to the importance of addressing perceptions of awareness and fairness in the design of such interactive systems.
