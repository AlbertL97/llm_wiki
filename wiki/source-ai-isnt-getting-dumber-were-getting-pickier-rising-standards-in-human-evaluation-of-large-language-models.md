# AI Isn't Getting Dumber, We're Getting Pickier: Rising Standards in Human Evaluation of Large Language Models

**Last updated:** 2026-11-11

## Overview

This paper investigates the common user perception that AI assistants, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), are declining in quality. Through an analysis of over a million user votes on the Chatbot Arena platform, the researchers present compelling evidence that the perceived degradation is not due to the AI becoming dumber, but rather due to rising human standards and expectations.

## Key Findings

*   **Increased "Both Are Bad" Votes:** Analysis of blind comparisons between fixed LLM versions revealed a consistent monthly increase in the percentage of votes where users found both options unsatisfactory. This trend held true across different time periods, languages, and model types.
*   **Stable Relative Rankings:** Despite the rise in absolute negative judgments, the relative performance rankings of the LLMs remained largely consistent. This suggests that while users' threshold for what constitutes an acceptable answer has increased, they can still effectively distinguish between superior and inferior models.
*   **Evaluation Drift Explained:** The study posits that this phenomenon is a form of 'evaluation drift,' where human standards naturally increase as the technology category progresses. A static product in a rapidly advancing field is therefore perceived as declining.
*   **Impact on AI Development:** The findings have critical implications for how LLMs are evaluated, particularly for leaderboards and for the human feedback loops used in model training, as these processes may be influenced by shifting user expectations rather than absolute AI performance.
