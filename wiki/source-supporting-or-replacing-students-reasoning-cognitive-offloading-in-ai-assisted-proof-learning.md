# Supporting or Replacing Students' Reasoning? Cognitive Offloading in AI-Assisted Proof Learning

This paper investigates the psychological impact of AI support systems on student learning, specifically focusing on cognitive offloading in an undergraduate mathematics course. The research involved 153 students who used an AI chatbot and a homework-feedback interface. Findings indicate that while the AI chatbot can improve homework scores, its excessive use is linked to lower exam performance and decreased self-efficacy, suggesting students may be offloading their reasoning processes. Conversely, a homework-feedback system requiring active submission did not yield negative performance outcomes.

## Key Findings

*   **Study Context:** 153 undergraduate students in a proof-based mathematics course were studied.
*   **AI Tool Components:** The AI support system included a chatbot and a homework-feedback interface.
*   **Initial Performance Gains:** Early access to the AI system led to higher homework scores.
*   **Exam Performance Discrepancy:** Gains in homework performance did not translate to significant improvements in subsequent exam scores.
*   **Negative Correlation with Chatbot Use:** Increased use of the AI chatbot was associated with lower exam scores.
*   **Self-Efficacy Impact:** A mediation analysis revealed that lower self-efficacy led to greater chatbot use, which in turn negatively impacted exam scores.
*   **Cognitive Offloading:** The study suggests a risk of students using chatbots to offload cognitive reasoning.
*   **Homework-Feedback Interface Effectiveness:** The homework-feedback interface, requiring submission of work, did not negatively affect exam performance.

## Recommendations

*   Advocates against the use of chatbots for AI tutoring.
*   Suggests exploring AI tutors that are integrated into course activities rather than standalone chatbots.
