# Source Summary: Same Question, Different Answers: Auditing Persona-Dependent Variation in Six LLMs on Smoking-Cessation Advice

## Overview

This paper examines the consistency of health advice provided by Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically focusing on smoking cessation. The research reveals that LLMs deliver different advice based on the user's persona, including variations in recommendations for cessation medications for teenagers versus adults, and differences in factual content presented to users with and without stated healthcare expertise. An intervention using system prompts to ensure a minimum factual content (a "factual floor") was tested, showing it could reduce persona-dependent variations, though its effectiveness varied across different LLMs.

## Key Findings

*   **Persona-Dependent Advice:** LLMs provide different smoking-cessation advice depending on the user persona's life stage, self-described expertise, and smoking history.
*   **Age-Related Discrepancies:** Teen personas received significantly less frequent mention of FDA-approved prescription cessation medications compared to adult personas across all audited LLMs.
*   **Expertise vs. Factual Content:** In most models, a persona claiming healthcare expertise received fewer factual details than a persona claiming no expertise.
*   **System Prompt Intervention:** A system prompt designed to enforce a "factual floor" helped to narrow persona-driven differences in the advice provided by LLMs.
*   **Model Specificity:** The effectiveness of the factual floor intervention varied across different LLMs, indicating that such prompts are not universally transferable.
*   **Clinical Nuance:** The study emphasizes that uniform factual delivery is not always clinically appropriate, advocating for age-aware factual floors (e.g., not recommending pharmacotherapy for minors).
*   **Complex Interactions:** The research acknowledges that personas have multiple varying attributes, and observed differences are at the scenario level rather than isolated effects of single attributes.
