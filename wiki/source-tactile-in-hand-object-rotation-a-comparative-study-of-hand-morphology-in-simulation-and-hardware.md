# Source Summary: Tactile In-Hand Object Rotation: A Comparative Study of Hand Morphology in Simulation and Hardware

This paper investigates the impact of hand morphology and tactile feedback on in-hand object rotation performance in robotic hands. The study compares an anthropomorphic LEAP Hand with a symmetric LEAP Hand, both equipped with vision-based tactile sensors (TacTip). Through simulations and hardware experiments, the research demonstrates that richer tactile information significantly enhances manipulation success, leading to increased rotation efficiency and reduced fingertip load. Furthermore, the findings reveal that the hand's physical design (morphology) plays a critical role in how contact is managed and how forces are distributed across fingers during manipulation. The symmetric hand, in particular, showed superior performance in rotation speed and count, along with more even load distribution, suggesting that geometric design combined with tactile sensing is key to advanced robotic manipulation.

## Key Findings

*   **Enhanced Rotation with Tactile Feedback:** Full tactile sensor observations dramatically improved in-hand object rotation performance in simulations compared to proprioception-only conditions. The anthropomorphic hand saw a 94.8% increase, and the symmetric hand a 66.9% increase.
*   **Morphology Impacts Finger Use and Load:**
    *   The anthropomorphic hand exhibited more varied finger participation.
    *   The symmetric hand distributed cumulative fingertip loading more evenly across its fingers.
*   **Symmetric Hand Superiority:** In hardware experiments, the symmetric hand achieved a significantly higher mean rotation count (32.0% more) and a higher aggregate rotation rate (25.8% more), while also demonstrating a 19.9% lower mean total fingertip load.
*   **Load Sharing and Contact Organization:** The study highlights how finger arrangement influences contact management and load sharing, crucial aspects for successful manipulation.
*   **Overall Performance:** Richer tactile information, coupled with appropriate hand geometry, facilitates greater task progress with lower overall force exertion.
