# Source Summary: Beyond Proxy Metrics: MLLMs as Human Surrogates for Explainable AI Evaluation

## Overview

This paper addresses the long-standing challenge in Explainable AI (XAI) of effectively evaluating explanation techniques. Traditional methods rely on either indirect proxy metrics, which offer limited insight into actual explanation utility, or human evaluations, which are resource-intensive, difficult to scale, and prone to reproducibility issues. The research explores the potential of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to act as automated human surrogates for evaluating XAI.

## Key Findings

*   **The Problem of XAI Evaluation:** Current evaluation methodologies for XAI are insufficient. Proxy metrics often fail to capture the true utility of explanations, while human evaluations, though considered the gold standard, are prohibitively expensive, hard to scale, and challenging to reproduce.
*   **MLLMs as Human Surrogates:** The study investigates whether MLLMs can be utilized as automated participants to evaluate XAI techniques, thereby overcoming the limitations of current methods.
*   **"Purpose-Grounded" Evaluation Framework:** A novel framework is proposed that uses "purpose-grounded" metrics applicable to both MLLMs and human users. This framework aims to provide a consistent basis for comparison.
*   **Trust and Applicability:** The paper offers conceptual insights into the conditions under which MLLMs can be trusted to serve as reliable surrogates for human evaluation.
*   **Empirical Validation:** Extensive empirical testing was conducted across five purpose-grounded areas, utilizing fourteen different MLLMs. The results demonstrated positive trends across all models.
*   **Recommended Models:** OpenAI and Qwen models showed higher alignment with human evaluation standards compared to other variants, making them the recommended choices for practical application in XAI evaluation.
*   **Complementary Approach:** The authors emphasize that this approach is intended to complement, not replace, traditional user testing, helping the field move beyond heavy reliance on proxy metrics.
