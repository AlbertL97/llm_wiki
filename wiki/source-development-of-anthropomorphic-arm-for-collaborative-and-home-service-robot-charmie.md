# Development of Anthropomorphic Arm for collaborative and Home Service Robot CHARMIE

## Introduction
This paper presents the development of an open-source, 3D-printed anthropomorphic arm intended for use in collaborative and home service robots. The work is situated within the context of rapid advancements in both service robotics and prosthetic technology, recognizing the growing trend of robots working alongside humans.

## Key Contributions

*   **Hardware Development:** The project resulted in the creation of a 3D-printed anthropomorphic arm.
*   **Control Systems:** Two distinct control methodologies were implemented:
    *   **Teleoperation:** Control through a camera feed combined with Machine Learning (ML)-based hand pose estimation.
    *   **Kinematic Control:** Utilizing the Denavit-Hartenberg method to define movement and calculate the end-effector's spatial position.
*   **Application Focus:** The arm is designed for integration into collaborative robots and home service robots, highlighting its potential for direct interaction and assistance in human environments.

## Significance
The development of such anthropomorphic manipulators is crucial for enhancing the capabilities of robots intended for close human-robot collaboration, particularly in domestic settings. The open-source nature of the project further facilitates research and development in this area.
