# No One to Blame: A Framework of Constitutive AI Unaccountability

**Last updated:** 2026-08-16

## Overview

This paper challenges the conventional view of AI accountability, proposing that certain AI system designs inherently lead to "constitutive AI unaccountability," making accountability impossible regardless of efforts in transparency or reform. The research identifies structural, technological, and normative factors that contribute to this phenomenon and develops a diagnostic tool to identify such accountability voids in deployed AI systems.

## Key Findings

*   **Constitutive AI Unaccountability:** Certain configurations of AI systems, actors, and institutions make accountability conceptually unattainable, irrespective of external measures.
*   **Nine Categories and 20 Themes:** The study identifies and categorizes factors contributing to unaccountability across structural, technological, and normative clusters.
*   **Interdependencies:** These factors reinforce each other through eight directed interdependencies.
*   **Diagnostic Instrument:** A 20-question diagnostic tool was developed to identify these accountability voids.
*   **Illustrative Application:** When applied to the OpenClaw AI system, the instrument detected 17 out of 20 conditions.
*   **Inverted Anthropomorphism:** A notable finding was an "inverted anthropomorphism" configuration where the AI agent was the sole identifiable actor, complicating traditional accountability.

## Contribution

The paper reframes AI unaccountability not as a solvable gap but as a constitutive property of sociotechnical systems. It extends existing barriers to accountability and provides a practical instrument for assessment.
