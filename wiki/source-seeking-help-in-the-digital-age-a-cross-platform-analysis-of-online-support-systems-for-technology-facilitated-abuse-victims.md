# Seeking Help in the Digital Age: A Cross-Platform Analysis of Online Support Systems for Technology-Facilitated Abuse Victims

## Introduction
Technology-facilitated abuse (TFA), defined as the use of digital technologies for stalking, harassment, monitoring, or threatening, is a prevalent form of interpersonal harm. Victims often turn to online resources for support, making the quality and nature of these responses crucial for their safety and understanding of the abuse.

## Methodology
This study conducted a comprehensive evaluation of online support systems for TFA victims across three distinct channels: 
*   **Web Search:** Simulated via Google Search.
*   **Peer-Support Forums:** Utilizing victim narratives from Reddit's r/Stalking over a decade.
*   **Conversational AI Systems:** Including general-purpose LLMs and specialized survivor-support chatbots.

Queries were derived from 11 categories of technology misuse identified from victim narratives. Responses were assessed using a unified framework examining:
*   **Core Response Quality:** Relevance, Accuracy, Actionability, Persuasiveness, Understandability.
*   **Platform Risks & Support Characteristics:** Social-engineering risk, toxicity, empathy, bias, risky guidance, and availability of concrete support information.

Automated classifiers were employed to facilitate large-scale analysis.

## Key Findings
*   **Platform Differences:** General-purpose LLMs and Google Search provided more relevant and actionable guidance than peer-support forums.
*   **Inconsistent Safety:** No evaluated platform consistently offered safe and trauma-informed support.
*   **Significant Risks:**
    *   Over 65% of search results contained potentially malicious links.
    *   More than 20% of Reddit discussions exhibited toxic responses.
    *   Conversational AI systems frequently lacked risk-aware guidance and concrete resources.
*   **Chatbot Performance:** Domain-specific survivor-support chatbots generally underperformed against general-purpose LLMs.

## Conclusion
The research reveals substantial deficiencies in the current digital ecosystem for supporting TFA victims. There is a pressing need to prioritize safety-centered design, rigorous evaluation, and responsible deployment for all future support technologies in this domain.
