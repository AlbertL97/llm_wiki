# Source Summary: Useful but Unwieldy: How Software Engineers Perceive the Design of Chain-of-Thought Explanations in Coding Agents

This paper investigates the perception and usability of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations provided by coding agents (CAs) to software engineers. The research highlights that while CoT messages are crucial for fostering transparency, scrutability, and trust between engineers and CAs, their practical implementation often leads to comprehension difficulties. Issues such as excessive verbosity, a mismatch in technical jargon, and a perceived "detached" conversational style where the agent seems to be "talking to itself" hinder effective use. The study, employing surveys and semi-structured interviews with 36 software engineers, ultimately aims to inform the design of more effective CoT mechanisms to meet user explainability needs.

## Key Findings

*   **Importance of CoT:** CoT explanations are vital for software engineers to understand the decision-making process of coding agents, thereby enhancing transparency, scrutability, and trust.
*   **Usability Challenges:
    *   **Verbosity:** Explanations are often too long.
    *   **Technical Mismatch:** The level of technical detail may not align with the user's expertise.
    *   **Conversational Detachment:** CoT messages can feel like the agent is communicating internally rather than with the user.
*   **Consequences of Challenges:** These issues lead to difficulties in comprehension and result in users selectively using or abandoning the CoT feature.
*   **Design Recommendations:** The findings provide insights for designing improved CoT mechanisms.

## Methodology

*   Qualitative research methods, including surveys and semi-structured interviews.
*   Thematic analysis was used to interpret the collected data.
*   Participants: 36 software engineers.
