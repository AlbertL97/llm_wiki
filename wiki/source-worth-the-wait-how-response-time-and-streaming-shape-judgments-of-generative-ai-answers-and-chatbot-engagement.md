# Worth the Wait? How Response Time and Streaming Shape Judgments of Generative AI Answers and Chatbot Engagement

## Introduction

Generative AI chatbots, particularly those based on large language models (LLMs), exhibit varying response times. Some models deliberate before replying (reasoning models), while others can deliver stored answers instantly (response caches). This study investigates how these differences in response time and delivery methods (streaming vs. instant presentation) influence user judgments of AI-generated answers and overall chatbot engagement.

## Key Findings

*   **Streaming Enhances Perceived Quality:** Answers that are streamed, meaning they unfold as if being written in real-time, are perceived more positively than identical answers presented instantly. This effect was observed in blind preference votes, where streamed answers were significantly more likely to be chosen over replayed, instantly presented answers.
*   **Negative Perceptions of Instant Replay:** When answers were replayed instantly, users rated them as more superficial, less complete, and more frequently incorrect.
*   **Latency Impacts Follow-up Engagement:** While direct preference votes might favor streaming, noticeable delays (latency) in response time, even if the answer is streamed, can negatively impact user behavior. Slower initial responses were found to make users less likely to ask follow-up questions.
*   **Real-world Implications:** The research highlights that the way an AI delivers its answer is as important as the answer itself. These findings are critical for developers designing AI services, influencing decisions on caching strategies, latency budgeting, and the evaluation metrics used for AI models.

## Methodology

The study employed naturalistic data from three sources:

1.  **ComparIA (French government chatbot arena):** Repeated prompts were answered either by a fresh, streamed answer or a stored, instantly replayed answer. Blind preference votes were collected.
2.  **Provider Congestion:** Data from 324,029 conversations were analyzed to observe the impact of slower streamed answers due to congestion on follow-up questions.
3.  **WildChat (Everyday ChatGPT use):** Data from 4,378,514 replies were examined to assess how congestion-induced delays affect user continuation of conversations.
