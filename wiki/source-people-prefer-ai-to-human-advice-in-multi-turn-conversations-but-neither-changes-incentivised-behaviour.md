# People prefer AI to human advice in multi-turn conversations but neither changes incentivised behaviour

This paper explores how people perceive advice given by AI chatbots versus human advisors, and whether this advice influences their decisions in incentivized tasks. The study found that while participants rated AI advisors as more rational, trustworthy, and convincing, this perceived advantage did not lead to changes in their behavior, specifically in donation decisions.

## Key Findings

*   **Preference for AI:** In multi-turn conversations, participants judged AI chatbot advisors to be significantly more rational, trustworthy, convincing, and sycophantic than human advisors.
*   **AI Engagement and Justification:** LLM advisors were observed to be more engaged in conversations, sharing more facts and more frequently stating opinions and justifying recommendations.
*   **Behavioral Invariance:** Despite the perceived differences in advisor quality, the donation decisions of participants did not vary across conditions (AI advice, human advice, no advice).
*   **AI Characteristics:** The research acknowledges the known issues with LLMs, such as sycophancy, overconfidence, and amplification of cognitive biases.
*   **Experimental Setup:** The study utilized a platform named 'Carrier' for conducting the experiment.
