# Source Summary: Curbside Chatbots: Auditing the Mental Health Interventions Large Language Models Recommend to Clinicians, Caregivers, and Patients

This paper, "Curbside Chatbots: Auditing the Mental Health Interventions Large Language Models Recommend to Clinicians, Caregivers, and Patients," examines the mental health advice provided by 41 large language models (LLMs). The study presented LLMs with 19 case vignettes, framing the queries from three distinct perspectives: a clinician describing a patient, a caregiver describing a loved one, and a person describing themselves. Across these scenarios, the researchers collected and analyzed 35,055 free-text responses to identify recommended interventions.

## Key Findings:

*   **Differential Recommendations:** LLMs exhibited significant differences in the mental health interventions they recommended based on the "footing" or perspective of the user (clinician, caregiver, or patient).
*   **Response Adaptation:** Models often provided a consultation-style response to clinicians, while offering more simplified and hedged advice to lay users.
*   **Self-Assessment Alignment:** There was a general tendency for models to recommend interventions that they themselves rated favorably, though this was not consistently applied, with some models recommending poorly rated options.
*   **Conditional Advice:** When prompted for specific intervention types, models frequently suggested options that they rated negatively, often appending conditions to these recommendations.
*   **Transparency and Future Work:** The researchers plan to release their audit instrument, code, and the full corpus as a "living audit" to facilitate ongoing research in this critical area.
