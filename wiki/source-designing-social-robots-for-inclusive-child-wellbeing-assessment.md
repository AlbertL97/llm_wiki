# Designing Social Robots for Inclusive Child Wellbeing Assessment: Insights from Communities Supporting Developmental Language Disorder and Forced Migration

## Introduction

Assessing the wellbeing and mental health of children can be particularly challenging for those facing communication barriers, such as children with Developmental Language Disorder (DLD) and children with forced migration backgrounds. Traditional self-report questionnaires, which rely heavily on language comprehension and verbal expression, are often inadequate for these populations. Social robots have emerged as a potential tool to facilitate wellbeing assessment by offering alternatives to purely verbal methods. However, there is a significant research gap concerning how these robot interactions can be designed to be inclusive, appropriate, and ethically sound for children with diverse communication needs.

## Methodology

To address this gap, the researchers developed candidate child-robot interaction activities to be used as design probes. They then conducted focus groups with parents and professionals who work with children with DLD and children from forced migration backgrounds. The qualitative data from these focus groups were analyzed using thematic analysis.

## Key Findings

Thematic analysis revealed several critical considerations for designing social robot interactions for child wellbeing assessment:

*   **Robot Role and Capabilities:** Understanding the perception of the robot's role (e.g., assessor, companion) and its functional capabilities is crucial.
*   **Interactional Dynamics:** The nuances of the interaction between the child and the robot, including non-verbal cues and engagement patterns, need careful consideration.
*   **Individual Differences:** Recognizing and accommodating the wide range of communication styles, sensitivities, and past experiences among children is essential.
*   **Child Agency:** Empowering children with a sense of control and autonomy during the assessment process is vital for ethical and effective interaction.
*   **Population-Specific Considerations:** Tailoring the design to address the unique communication challenges and lived experiences of children with DLD (e.g., specific language processing difficulties) and children with forced migration backgrounds (e.g., potential trauma, cultural differences) is paramount.

## Design Recommendations and Contributions

Based on these findings, the study derives a set of ethical and inclusive design recommendations for robot-mediated wellbeing assessments. This work contributes valuable design guidance for developing social robots that can effectively and sensitively support the wellbeing assessment of children with diverse communication needs, moving beyond the limitations of traditional methods.
