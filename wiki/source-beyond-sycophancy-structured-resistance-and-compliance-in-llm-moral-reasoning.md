# Beyond Sycophancy: Structured Resistance and Compliance in LLM Moral Reasoning

## Main Findings

This research explores how Large Language Models (LLMs) engage in moral reasoning and update their judgments when presented with differing perspectives. Moving beyond the concept of simple sycophancy (excessive agreement), the study proposes that LLMs exhibit a "structured resistance and compliance" process. This process mirrors phenomena in human social psychology, where judgment revision is influenced by:

*   **View Distance:** The proximity of an incoming view to the model's current position.
*   **Source Attribution:** Whether the incoming view is presented as the model's own prior judgment or attributed to an external source.
*   **Coalition Structure:** The dynamics of group pressure or support for a particular viewpoint.

The findings indicate that LLMs are more likely to revise their judgments when the new information is conceptually close, when it appears to align with their own prior reasoning, and when influenced by group dynamics. This work reframes sycophancy as one facet of a more complex social influence process in LLMs, offering a foundation for developing AI that can constructively engage with diverse viewpoints without uncritical compliance, particularly in morally sensitive applications.
