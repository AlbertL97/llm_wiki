# Teacher Knows It Best: Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking and Tipping Points in Networked Langevin Dynamics AI Sycophancy

## Overview

This paper introduces a statistical physics framework to model the phenomenon of AI sycophancy, where AI systems like Large Language Models (LLMs) can reinforce inaccurate beliefs within a society, leading to a "delusional spiraling" effect. The authors develop a networked Langevin dynamics model to capture this bistable system driven by noise and social conformity. They propose an intervention strategy using "aware" nodes, termed "Teachers," placed at critical network hubs, to counteract these negative feedback loops.

## Key Findings

*   **AI Sycophancy Model:** A statistical physics model based on networked Langevin dynamics is formulated to represent AI sycophancy and its potential to induce "delusional spiraling" in social systems.
*   **Bistability and Tipping Points:** The model exhibits bistability and analyzes critical tipping points in the network's dynamics, suggesting a phase transition in belief formation influenced by AI.
*   **Teacher Intervention:** A minority of "aware" nodes (Teachers) strategically placed at topological hubs can effectively disrupt or reverse the sycophantic reinforcement process.
*   **Analytical Solutions:** The paper provides a closed-form analytical derivation for the critical tipping time via a saddle-node bifurcation, validated through finite-size scaling and data collapse across different network topologies.
*   **Optimal Intervention Strategy:** An optimization framework demonstrates that concentrated, rapid interventions targeting influential network hubs are more effective than distributed, slow interventions for mitigating negative AI-driven societal effects, under budget constraints.
