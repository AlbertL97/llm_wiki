# Rethinking Legibility in Social Robot Hallway Navigation: Impact of Intent Representation and Human Distraction

This paper explores the concept of **legibility** in the context of social robot navigation, specifically focusing on how robots can generate motion that clearly communicates their intentions to humans, even in dynamic environments where human attention might be divided. The research conducted two user studies to evaluate different methods of intent representation and the impact of human distraction on navigation performance and perceptions.

## Key Findings:

*   **Intent Representation Matters:** The way a robot's intent is represented significantly impacts human perception and interaction. Specifically, **passing-side legibility**, which focuses on how a robot intends to pass a pedestrian, proved more effective than destination-based or non-legible approaches.
*   **Adaptive Legibility:** Dynamically updating the passing-side legibility representation further enhanced its effectiveness, leading to smoother human motion and perceptions of the robot being more competent and less mentally and physically demanding.
*   **Legibility Aids Under Distraction:** The study demonstrated that legible robot motion allows humans to navigate more smoothly even when their attention is divided or distracted. This benefit persists to some extent even if subjective ratings don't fully capture it.
*   **Importance for Social Navigation:** The findings highlight the need for interaction-level intent representations in social robot navigation to facilitate coordination and safe interaction, especially in challenging environments like hallways.
