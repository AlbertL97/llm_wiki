# What's the Difference? Voice Design and Confirmation Bias in AI-Assisted Self-Diagnosis: Anthropomorphic vs. Machine-Like Voice Output

**Last updated:** 2026-11-17

## Overview
This paper explores the impact of AI voice design on confirmation bias within AI-assisted self-diagnosis systems. As AI symptom checkers become more prevalent, understanding their influence on user decision-making, especially concerning trust and cognitive biases like confirmation bias, is critical. The study specifically investigates whether using an anthropomorphic (human-like) versus a machine-like (xenomorphic) voice for the AI affects users' susceptibility to confirmation bias when receiving diagnostic recommendations.

## Key Findings

*   **AI Recommendations Drive Bias, Not Voice Style:** The study found a significant influence of the AI's recommendation direction on user self-diagnosis. Participants were far more likely to change their diagnosis when the AI contradicted their initial choice (45.7% of trials) than when it confirmed it (1.2% of trials).
*   **Impact on Confidence:** User confidence in their diagnosis increased when the AI's recommendation aligned with their initial choice, but showed minimal change when the recommendation contradicted it.
*   **Decision Latency:** Participants took longer to make their final diagnostic decision after receiving a contradictory recommendation from the AI.
*   **Voice Design Insignificant:** Despite participants clearly perceiving the anthropomorphic and machine-like voices as distinct, the *type* of voice used did not significantly alter the observed confirmation bias effects. Neither the voice style itself nor any interaction between voice style and recommendation direction proved significant.
*   **Conclusion:** The primary factor influencing confirmation bias in this AI-assisted self-diagnosis context is the directional nature of the AI's feedback, rather than the degree of anthropomorphism in its voice.
