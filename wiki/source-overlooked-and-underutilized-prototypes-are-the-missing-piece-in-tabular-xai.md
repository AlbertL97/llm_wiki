# Overlooked and Underutilized: Prototypes are the Missing Piece in Tabular XAI

## Abstract

This paper argues for the integration of prototypes, particularly "prototypical parts," as a more intuitive and cognitively efficient method for explaining complex machine learning models when working with tabular data. Current XAI methods often rely heavily on feature attribution, which can be overwhelming for non-expert users due to the cognitive load required to process numerous isolated features. The authors propose that by grouping related features into semantic units, prototypes can embody human-understandable concepts, thereby reducing cognitive load and aligning with human cognitive processes like categorization and intuitive thinking. This approach, supported by cognitive theories and a new validation framework (Geometric Validity Triad), aims to make tabular XAI genuinely comprehensible by complementing feature-level auditing with similarity-based reasoning.

## Key Findings

*   **Critique of Feature Attribution:** Existing feature attribution methods in tabular XAI are criticized for demanding excessive cognitive effort from users.
*   **Value of Prototypes:** Prototypes, especially "prototypical parts," are identified as a crucial but underutilized element for improving XAI.
*   **Prototypical Parts Defined:** These are conceptualized as bounded subspaces grouping related features into semantic units.
*   **Cognitive Alignment:** Prototypes are linked to theories of human cognition, specifically Rosch's categorization theory and Kahneman's System 1 thinking, to explain their ability to reduce cognitive load.
*   **Geometric Validity Triad:** A novel framework is proposed for conceptualizing the validation of data manifolds through prototypes.
*   **Future Direction:** The paper advocates for composite explanation pipelines that blend prototype-based reasoning with feature-level analysis for enhanced comprehension.
