# Source Summary: From Values to Benchmarks: Evaluating Large Language Models for Governmental Use in Dutch

This paper introduces the "Grip on LLMs" framework, a systematic evaluation suite designed for assessing Large Language Models (LLMs) in Dutch governmental contexts. It moves beyond purely technical performance to include dimensions crucial for public administration, such as factuality, honesty, and social bias, alongside environmental impact and cost. The research highlights that no single LLM excels across all measured dimensions and, critically, that factuality does not guarantee honesty. This distinction is important for building trust in AI systems used by the public sector.

## Key Findings

*   Developed the "Grip on LLMs" framework for evaluating LLMs in governmental settings.
*   Identified six key evaluation dimensions: factuality, honesty, social bias, energy consumption, cost, and training data transparency.
*   Tested over 30 multilingual and Dutch-specific LLMs using the framework.
*   Found unavoidable trade-offs between LLM quality, environmental impact, and financial cost.
*   Observed that bias in LLMs appears largely independent of cost and environmental factors.
*   Crucially, demonstrated that factuality (correctness) and honesty (acknowledging limitations) are distinct properties, meaning high factuality does not imply high honesty.
*   Created a user-friendly model overview for non-technical stakeholders to aid in LLM selection.
