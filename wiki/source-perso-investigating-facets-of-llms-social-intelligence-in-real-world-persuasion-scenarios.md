# PerSo: Investigating Facets of LLMs' Social Intelligence in Real-World Persuasion Scenarios

## Introduction
This paper addresses the growing importance of understanding the social intelligence of Large Language Models (LLMs) for their deployment in human-AI interactions and multi-agent systems. Social intelligence is defined by two core components: cognitive intelligence, which involves inferring others' mental states, and behavioral intelligence, which pertains to selecting appropriate social actions to achieve social objectives based on those inferences. The authors argue that persuasion scenarios are ideal for evaluating both aspects simultaneously, a contrast to prior work that often isolated these components using artificial settings.

## PerSo Benchmark
To bridge this gap, the researchers introduce PerSo, a new benchmark specifically designed to assess LLMs' social intelligence in real-world persuasion contexts. The benchmark is constructed using authentic persuasion dialogues extracted from Reddit's "ChangeMyView" community. A key aspect of the methodology involved an LLM-in-the-loop pipeline for task creation, ensuring that the evaluation tasks were grounded in realistic interaction patterns and further validated through human annotations.

## Key Findings
*   **Dual-Aspect Evaluation:** PerSo enables the evaluation of both the cognitive (inferring mental states) and behavioral (choosing appropriate actions) dimensions of LLM social intelligence.
*   **Model Size Correlation:** The study observed that the performance of LLMs on the PerSo benchmark generally correlates positively with their size, with larger models exhibiting better performance.
*   **Differential Performance:** LLM capabilities vary across the different facets of social intelligence assessed.
*   **Human-LLM Comparison:** While the leading LLM in the study demonstrated slightly superior performance to the best human participants on the behavioral persuasion task, human participants retained an edge in the cognitive task of inferring mental states.
*   **Ecological Validity:** The use of real-world data from "ChangeMyView" and human validation efforts contribute to the benchmark's ecological validity, making it a more robust measure of LLM social intelligence in practical settings.

## Conclusion
The PerSo benchmark and the findings derived from it offer valuable insights into the current state of LLM social intelligence. They underscore both the advancements made in emulating human-like social behavior and the remaining challenges, particularly in areas requiring deep cognitive understanding of human mental states, which are critical for effective and trustworthy human-AI collaboration.
