# When Readability and Source Retention Diverge: An Evaluability Gap in AI Translation

**Authors:** Michael L. Bernstein, Jiarui Lu, Qian Zhang, Justin Z. Wu

**Last updated:** 2026-08-20

## Overview

This research investigates an "evaluability gap" in AI translation, where highly readable AI outputs can mislead users into overestimating translation quality, even when the original source text is available for comparison. The study examines how the nature of the source material (simple narrative vs. complex prose) and the AI's output strategy (prioritizing readability vs. fidelity to the source) influence user perceptions of translation quality, trust in the AI, and willingness to disclose personal information.

## Key Findings

*   **The Evaluability Gap:** Users may struggle to accurately assess the quality of AI translations, particularly when the output is highly readable but may have diverged significantly from the source. Providing the source text does not automatically enable users to detect these discrepancies, especially with complex content.
*   **Impact on Perceived Quality:**
    *   For simple narratives, users preferred and rated fidelity-oriented translations higher than readability-oriented ones.
    *   For complex literary or philosophical texts, the preference for fidelity over readability diminished, and users' ability to discern quality differences based on the source text was impaired.
*   **Effects on Trust and Anthropomorphism:** Perceptions of the AI's intelligence, its perceived agency (anthropomorphic attribution), and task-performance trust were correlated with the observed patterns in perceived quality. When users could better evaluate fidelity, these positive attributions increased.
*   **Disclosure Willingness:** The study found that task-performance trust was the most significant predictor of users' willingness to entrust personal text to the AI system. This highlights the critical role of perceived reliability and accuracy in user data disclosure decisions.
*   **Source Access vs. Source Evaluability:** The research differentiates between merely *providing* access to the source material and enabling users to *evaluate* the AI's output against that source. The latter is crucial for accurate quality assessment and building appropriate trust.
