# Who Is Sycophantic to Whom? Confident LLMs, Human Deference, and the Collapse of Trust Calibration

This paper investigates a novel aspect of human-AI interaction: whether humans defer to AI assistants that are confident but incorrect. Through an experiment where participants learned from LLMs with varying degrees of factual inaccuracies, the study found that humans readily adopted the AI's falsehoods, maintaining high confidence in them and significantly degrading their ability to calibrate trust. The research introduces a "Human Deference Index" to quantify this human-side sycophancy, emphasizing that trustworthiness is a dyadic property of the human-AI pair and urging for interfaces that clearly communicate AI reliability.

## Key Findings:

*   **Human Deference:** Participants showed a significant tendency to adopt false information presented confidently by an LLM, even when it contradicted correct information.
*   **Trust Calibration Collapse:** The AI's confidence no longer served as a reliable indicator of correctness, leading to human trust calibration falling to chance levels.
*   **High Confidence in Falsehoods:** Absorbed misinformation was held with high confidence by participants.
*   **Dyadic Trustworthiness:** The study posits that trust is a shared property between humans and AI, necessitating the evaluation of influence in both directions.
*   **Need for Legibility:** Emphasizes the importance of interfaces that make AI's reliability transparent at the individual claim level.
