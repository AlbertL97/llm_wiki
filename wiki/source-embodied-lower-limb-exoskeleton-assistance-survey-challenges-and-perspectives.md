# Embodied Lower-limb Exoskeleton Assistance: Survey, Challenges, and Perspectives

This review paper surveys the field of embodied lower-limb exoskeletons, offering a structured analysis beyond typical mechanical or algorithmic focuses. It categorizes existing research into four key dimensions: structural self-adaptation, task adaptation, user-specific adaptation, and control self-regulation. The paper highlights critical challenges such as achieving lightweight and compliant designs, effective data fusion, optimizing complex control policies, and ensuring robust control. A central theme is the embodied perspective, emphasizing the closed-loop interplay between an exoskeleton's mechanical structure, sensing capabilities, and control systems as a framework for understanding and advancing human-robot interaction. The identified future directions aim towards developing more comfortable, practical, and effective physical assistance technologies.
