# Vision-Language Models for Egocentric Video: From Hand-Object Interaction to Embodied AI

This paper surveys the application of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to understanding egocentric video data. Egocentric video, captured from a first-person perspective, is highly valuable for AI research in areas such as human-robot interaction (HRI) and embodied AI, offering direct insight into human actions, intentions, and interactions with objects. The survey reviews how VLMs leverage visual and textual information to overcome challenges inherent in egocentric data, like ego-motion and occlusions.

## Key Findings

*   **Evolving Role of VLMs:** VLMs are crucial for bridging the gap between raw visual egocentric data and semantic understanding, moving beyond simple object recognition towards comprehending complex interactions.
*   **Applications in Embodied AI:** The research highlights how egocentric video processed by VLMs can significantly enhance robot skill learning, facilitate human-to-robot knowledge transfer, and improve embodied decision-making systems.
*   **Challenges and Limitations:** Despite advancements, current VLMs struggle with understanding dynamic interactions, recognizing evolving actions, and inferring user intent over long sequences of egocentric video.
*   **Future Directions:** Critical areas for future research include developing more robust temporal reasoning, interaction-aware supervision, efficient long-video processing, advanced multimodal fusion techniques, and ensuring privacy and trustworthiness in embodied AI systems.
