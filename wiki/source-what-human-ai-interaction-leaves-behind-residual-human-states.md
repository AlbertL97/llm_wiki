# Source Summary: What Human-AI Interaction Leaves Behind: Residual Human States -- A Trajectory-Based Framework and Multi-Study Analysis

## Overview

This paper introduces a novel framework called **Residual Human States (RHS)** to analyze the lasting psychological and behavioral impacts of human-AI interaction beyond immediate task performance. The authors argue that current evaluations often overlook how interacting with AI can alter a user's state in subsequent, unaided actions. RHS is presented as a trajectory-based model that captures these persistent changes relative to alternative interaction pathways.

## Key Findings & Propositions

*   **The Concept of Residual Human States:** Interaction with AI can lead to human states that persist after the interaction ends, affecting future behavior and cognition.
*   **Distinction Between Assisted Performance and Human-State Transition:** The framework emphasizes that improvements in performance *during* AI assistance do not automatically equate to positive or lasting changes in the human user's underlying capabilities or biases.
*   **Four Core Propositions:**
    *   **Multidimensionality:** Residual states can impact various aspects of human cognition and behavior.
    *   **Interaction-Policy Dependence:** The specific design and policy of the AI interaction significantly shape these residual effects.
    *   **Horizon Dissociation:** The impact of AI interaction may manifest differently at various time points after the interaction ceases, not just immediately.
    *   **Temporal Persistence:** These residual states can endure over extended periods.
*   **Empirical Evidence:** The paper supports these propositions with analyses from four distinct empirical cases, including medical training simulations, diagnostic learning, generative AI tutoring in classrooms, and longitudinal studies of personalized AI dialogue. These cases demonstrate how AI interactions can subtly alter decision-making, learning, and performance in subsequent unaided tasks.

## Implications

The research advocates for a more comprehensive evaluation of AI systems that accounts for their long-term influence on human users, considering not just immediate utility but also the enduring psychological and behavioral consequences of interaction.
