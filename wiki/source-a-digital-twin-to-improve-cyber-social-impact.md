# A Digital Twin to Improve Cyber-Social Impact

## Overview

This paper introduces a novel framework designed to address the limitations of traditional cybersecurity models in assessing the full impact of cyberattacks on critical infrastructures. It proposes the integration of Digital Twin technology with a Cyber-Social Security model to simulate, analyze, and quantify threats, moving beyond purely technical metrics to include societal and operational consequences.

## Key Findings and Contributions

*   **Framework for Cyber-Social Impact Assessment:** The core contribution is a Digital Twin-based framework that enhances cybersecurity analysis by incorporating social dimensions.
*   **Integration of Technologies:** Leverages Digital Twin technology, virtualized ICS environments (Mininet, MiniCPS), and LLMs for threat simulation and analysis.
*   **Cyber-Social Vulnerability Scoring System (CSVSS):** Introduces a new quantitative metric that extends existing vulnerability scoring systems (like CVSS) by adding a social impact dimension, providing a more comprehensive severity score.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Design:** Emphasizes the critical role of human analysts in the risk assessment process. An LLM performs initial analysis and reporting, but human oversight and decision-making authority are preserved, supported by explainable AI (XAI) feedback.
*   **Focus on Critical Infrastructures:** The framework is specifically aimed at improving the understanding and management of cyber threats in critical infrastructures.
*   **Demonstrated Efficacy:** Experimental results show the framework's ability to model and score cyber-social impacts effectively.

## Methodology

*   **Simulation:** Virtualized ICS environments are used to model attack scenarios.
*   **Analysis:** LLMs assist in initial vulnerability identification and report generation.
*   **Scoring:** The CSVSS provides a quantitative measure of cyber-social impact.
*   **Validation:** XAI supports human analysts in validating LLM outputs.
*   **Decision Making:** Human analysts retain ultimate decision authority.
