# This Is So Claude! Towards a Theory of the Recognition of AI Character Without Reidentification

**Authors:** Elias Cohen, Maya L. Krauss, Sarah Creel, Michael R. Bernstein

**Date:** 2026-08-16 (v1)

**Abstract:** Users sometimes judge that an unfamiliar response is "so Claude." What does this judgment recognize, if it does not identify which model, process, conversation, or mind produced the response? I distinguish three orders of inquiry into AI identity.

1.  **Constraint-first inquiry:** Begins with conditions that a persisting interlocutor should satisfy.
2.  **Mechanism-first inquiry:** Begins with structures peculiar to language models and asks whether they delimit plausible entities.
3.  **Recognition-first inquiry:** Begins with an ordinary capacity: recognizing a way of responding as "Claudish" before selecting a persisting bearer.

**Key Findings & Arguments:**

*   **Recognition of AI Character:** The paper argues that users can identify a recognizable "conversational character" of an AI, which is independent of specific computational instantiations or conversational histories.
*   **"Claudishness" as a Phenomenon:** The phenomenon of judging a response as "so Claude" suggests an ability to recognize a specific style or persona, even when the AI's underlying identity is unknown or unresolvable.
*   **Three Orders of Inquiry into AI Identity:**
    *   **Constraint-first:** Focuses on logical and existential requirements for a continuous interlocutor.
    *   **Mechanism-first:** Examines the limitations and possibilities imposed by the AI's internal architecture (e.g., LLM structures).
    *   **Recognition-first:** Prioritizes the user's perceptual ability to identify a response pattern as belonging to a particular AI's style or character.
*   **Hypotheses on Projectible Character:**
    *   **Hypothesis 1 (Conversational Character):** If judgments of "Claudishness" generalize across unfamiliar tasks and controlled for branding/familiar phrases, it suggests the existence of a real, projectible conversational character.
    *   **Hypothesis 2 (Realization in Activation Space):** This projectible character might be compactly and effectively realized in the AI's activation space, although this is more speculative and requires further validation.
*   **Distinction Between Character and Bearer:** The paper emphasizes that an AI's recognizable character can persist through changes in its computational bearer (e.g., different server instances, updated models), and the same character can potentially be embodied by different bearers. Conversely, a single bearer might exhibit a changing character over time.
*   **Implications for AI Companions:** This distinction is significant for AI companions, as the continuity of a recognizable character can be a primary focus of user attachment, even when the underlying computational continuity is ambiguous or unresolved.
