Title: Evaluating Explainable AI Methods for Geoscientific Regression: Insights from Applications and the Lorenz-63 System

Summary:
As AI systems are increasingly deployed in operational roles within Earth system science and forecasting, establishing trust in their predictions is paramount. However, the inherent complexity of modern AI models often renders them 'black boxes,' making their internal decision-making processes difficult to interpret. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques aim to address this by providing insights into these processes. A significant challenge arises because most XAI methods have been developed for classification tasks, and their efficacy for the regression problems prevalent in geoscientific applications is not well-established.

This paper systematically reviews XAI approaches, organizing them into a structured framework that examines their theoretical underpinnings and practical behavior, specifically within the context of regression. To concretely illustrate these points, the authors apply a selection of XAI methods to a machine learning emulator of the Lorenz 1963 system, a chaotic model chosen for its tractability and physical meaningfulness. This case study serves to highlight the limitations and failure modes of general-purpose XAI when applied to regression problems.

The research further surveys the application of these and related XAI methods across various Earth system science disciplines. It also situates XAI within the broader model development lifecycle, connecting methodological choices to the diverse needs of different stakeholder groups in operational Earth system science.

Finally, the paper identifies current gaps in XAI methodologies and outlines a forward-looking research agenda. This agenda includes practical recommendations for the responsible and effective application of XAI in geoscientific modeling and forecasting, particularly for regression-based tasks.

Key Findings:
- Trust in AI predictions is crucial for operational geoscientific applications.
- Existing XAI methods are largely designed for classification, posing challenges for their use in regression tasks common in geosciences.
- A systematic review and framework for evaluating XAI in regression contexts are needed.
- Application to the Lorenz 63 system demonstrates limitations of general-purpose XAI in regression.
- XAI needs to be integrated within the model development lifecycle and tailored to stakeholder needs.
- A research agenda is proposed to advance XAI for geoscientific regression, focusing on responsible and effective use.
