## Supporting or Replacing Students' Reasoning? Cognitive Offloading in AI-Assisted Proof Learning

### Key Findings:

*   **Study Design:** 153 undergraduate students in a proof-based mathematics course participated in a study examining the use of an AI support system with staggered access to a chatbot and a homework-feedback interface.
*   **Early Access Effects:** Students with earlier access to the AI chatbot and homework-feedback interface showed higher homework scores (2.56 points higher) during the initial phase.
*   **Exam Performance:** Despite improved homework scores, exam performance did not significantly differ between early and late access groups during the staggered access period. This suggests that AI-supported homework improvements did not translate to better exam results.
*   **Chatbot Use and Exam Scores:** After all students gained access, each additional 100 messages sent to the AI chatbot correlated with a 3.02-point decrease in subsequent exam scores.
*   **Mediation Analysis:** Lower baseline self-efficacy was indirectly linked to lower exam scores through increased chatbot use. A one-point decrease in self-efficacy was associated with a 0.62-point drop in exam scores via chatbot usage.
*   **Cognitive Offloading Concern:** The findings raise concerns that students might use the chatbot to offload their cognitive reasoning processes rather than engaging in them.
*   **Homework-Feedback Interface:** In contrast to the chatbot, the homework-feedback interface, which required students to submit proof attempts before receiving feedback, did not show negative associations with exam performance.
*   **Recommendations:** The study recommends against using chatbots for AI tutoring and suggests exploring tutors that are embedded within the course activities. The findings highlight potential pitfalls of AI tools that facilitate passive learning or cognitive offloading.
