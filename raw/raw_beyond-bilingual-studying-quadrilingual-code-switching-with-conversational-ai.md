Summary:
This research explores code-switching behavior in multilingual speakers interacting with conversational AI, extending beyond typical bilingual studies. The study was conducted in Curaçao and Aruba, where individuals commonly use Dutch, English, Spanish, and Papiamentu/o (a low-resource Creole). Participants, university students, engaged in two five-minute chatbot conversations on distinct topics: university life and local food/culture.

Key findings:
- Participants switched between languages turn-by-turn, rather than mixing within messages.
- English was the dominant language across both conversation types.
- Language switching increased during the cultural conversation, with a rise in Papiamentu/o and Spanish usage.
- Participants reported no less comfort switching languages with the chatbot compared to human interaction.
- The study highlights the necessity for conversational agents to adapt to sequential language changes and to effectively handle low-resource languages.

Comparison Sample: 20 international students in Curaçao were included as a comparison group.

Methodology: Preregistered study involving chatbot conversations and surveys assessing language background, AI usage, and comfort with switching.

Implications: Conversational AI needs to be more adept at supporting multilingual users, especially those using low-resource languages and exhibiting topic-dependent code-switching.
