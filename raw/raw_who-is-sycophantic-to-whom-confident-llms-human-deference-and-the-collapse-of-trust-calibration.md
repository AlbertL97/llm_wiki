# Who Is Sycophantic to Whom? Confident LLMs, Human Deference, and the Collapse of Trust Calibration

**Authors:** [Not specified in the provided text]
**Date:** 2026-10-24 (based on URL format)

## Main Findings:

*   **Human Deference to Incorrect AI:** Contrary to expectations of AI sycophancy, humans tend to "bend" towards confident, consistent, and incorrect Large Language Models (LLMs).
*   **Experiment Design:** 32 participants learned science topics from five AI assistants with varying percentages (0-100%) of factual information replaced by plausible falsehoods. All assistants presented information with full confidence.
*   **Absorption of Falsehoods:** Participants adopted the AI's falsehoods 60% of the time on falsified items, holding these absorbed falsehoods with high confidence (8.4/10).
*   **Erosion of Trust Calibration:** The AI's confidence advantage over correct answers vanished, and the ability to discriminate between correct and incorrect information based on confidence dropped to chance levels (AUROC 0.70 to chance).
*   **Trust Ratings Unchanged:** Post-task trust ratings for the AI barely changed despite the significant degradation in performance and trust calibration.
*   **Returning to the Chat:** Participants tended to return to the chat more when the assistant performed worse, but this correlated with increased absorption of falsehoods, not reduced reliance.
*   **Quiz Questions to AI:** 30% of participant turns involved pasting quiz questions back to the AI being checked.
*   **AI Behavior:** The AI assistants were largely not sycophantic and did not concede under pushback.
*   **Human Deference Index:** A new metric, the "baseline-corrected Human Deference Index," is introduced to quantify human-side sycophancy.
*   **Dyadic Trustworthiness:** The research argues that trustworthiness is a property of the human-AI pair and that sycophancy evaluations should consider both directions of influence.
*   **Legibility of Reliability:** Findings highlight the need for learning interfaces to make the reliability of AI claims legible on a claim-by-claim basis.
