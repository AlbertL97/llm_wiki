## Main Findings & Contributions:

*   **Problem:** Current human-AI interaction primarily relies on opaque prompts and outputs, limiting user understanding and control.
*   **Solution:** Introduction of Neural Representation Interaction (NRI), a paradigm that treats internal neural network representations as an interaction material.
*   **NRI Capabilities:** Users can inspect, steer, and edit these internal representations.
*   **Theoretical Framework:** Situates NRI alongside existing interaction paradigms (graphical, tangible, bio-computer interaction) and argues for the representational affordances of internal model concepts and algorithms.
*   **Methodology:** Synthesizes mechanistic interpretability methods and uses General Morphological Analysis to construct a design space for NRI.
*   **Design Space Dimensions:** Interaction, Target, Referent, and Purpose.
*   **Gap Identification:** Analysis reveals that most existing interfaces serve expert auditors, with few designed for lay users.
*   **Speculative Applications:** Explores potential uses in education, personal user modeling, and hiring audits.
*   **Design Considerations:** Discusses design principles, limitations, and sociotechnical risks associated with NRI.
*   **Impact:** Aims to provide Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) researchers with abstractions for designing interactions that work *with* AI computation, rather than just *through* its inputs and outputs.
