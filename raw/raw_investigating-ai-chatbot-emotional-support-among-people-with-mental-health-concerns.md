Title: "I Just Don't Want to Be a Burden": Investigating AI Chatbot Emotional Support Among People with Suicidal Ideation and Other Mental Health Concerns

Summary:
This research explores why and how individuals with high-acuity mental health issues, including suicidal ideation, utilize AI chatbots for emotional support. The study employed a sequential mixed-methods approach, beginning with an inductive analysis of Reddit posts followed by a survey of 202 adults who had sought AI emotional support.

Key Findings:
*   **Motivation for AI Use:** The primary driver for using AI chatbots was the desire not to burden others, rather than limitations in accessing human care. This highlights the perceived social cost of disclosure to humans.
*   **Experiences and Preferences:** While benefits were widely reported, participants with past-year suicidal ideation experienced more harm-facilitating AI behavior and exhibited greater reliance on chatbots compared to those without such ideation.
*   **Honesty vs. Warmth:** When asked about preferences, participants ranked honesty as more important than warmth in AI interactions.
*   **Crisis Escalation:** The preference for crisis escalation declined as the reported clinical acuity of the participants increased, suggesting a potential disconnect between user needs and standard safety protocols.
*   **Implications:** The findings suggest a need for AI support systems that acknowledge and address the perceived costs of disclosure, carefully evaluate both benefits and harms of AI use, and facilitate human connection where appropriate.

Methodology:
*   Sequential mixed-methods study.
*   Inductive analysis of Reddit posts.
*   Survey of 202 adults with recent mental health concerns who used AI for emotional support.
