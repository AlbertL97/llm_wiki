## Description

Egocentric video, capturing events from the wearer's viewpoint, offers rich insights into human attention, hand-object interactions, and goal-directed behaviors. This perspective is crucial for emerging fields like wearable intelligence, assistive systems, human-robot interaction (HRI), and embodied AI. However, it presents unique challenges such as ego-motion, occlusions, small active objects, viewpoint-dependent appearances, and long-range temporal dependencies.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are identified as a promising approach to address these challenges by integrating visual observations with semantic knowledge and natural language supervision.

## Key Contributions and Review Areas

This survey critically reviews VLMs applied to egocentric video understanding, charting their evolution from traditional recognition architectures to advanced multimodal foundation models and embodied systems.

## Identified Limitations and Future Priorities

A consistent limitation observed is that current models excel at recognizing visible objects but struggle with understanding evolving interactions, actions, and user intent, particularly over extended activities.

**Key priorities for deployable embodied intelligence include:**
*   Temporally grounded reasoning
*   Interaction-aware supervision
*   Efficient long-video processing
*   Multimodal fusion
*   Graph-enhanced representations
*   Cross-domain generalization
*   Privacy
*   Trustworthy evaluation
