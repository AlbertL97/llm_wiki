# GenAI-DBT: Evaluating a DBT-Informed Generative AI Platform for Mindfulness Skills Training: A Pilot Randomized Study

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided text.
**Date:** 2026-11-22 (based on URL date)

## Summary Details:

This study investigated the feasibility, acceptability, and engagement of three digital formats for Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) mindfulness skills training among university students.

### Methodology:

*   **Design:** A 30-day pilot randomized study.
*   **Participants:** 45 university students.
*   **Conditions:** Participants were assigned to one of three groups:
    *   GenAI-DBT: A DBT-informed generative AI platform.
    *   Chatbot: A general-purpose large language model chatbot.
    *   Web: A static web resource.
*   **Completion:** 42 participants completed the study; 9 participated in follow-up interviews.

### Key Findings:

*   **Usability:** Usability scores were within acceptable ranges across all conditions, with no significant between-group differences.
*   **Adherence:** GenAI-DBT demonstrated the highest observed participant-week adherence (71.4%), compared to the Web resource (53.3%) and the Chatbot (42.3%). This difference was descriptive.
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** The Mindful Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS) and Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS) scores showed no significant time-by-group interactions. Session-level self-reported mood changes also did not differ significantly across conditions.
*   **Linguistic Analysis:** GenAI-DBT showed a significantly larger positive change in linguistic valence than the Chatbot (4.28 vs. 1.59, P = .02, Cohen's d = 0.99).
*   **Qualitative Feedback:** Interviewees appreciated contextualized, scenario-based practice. Areas identified for improvement included response latency, repetition, and limited personalization.

### Conclusion:

The findings suggest that DBT-informed conversational AI is feasible and acceptable for skills practice. Further evaluation in larger, more clinically relevant samples is recommended, as clinical efficacy has not yet been established.
