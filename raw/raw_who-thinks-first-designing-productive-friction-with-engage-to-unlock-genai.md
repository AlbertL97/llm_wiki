## Engage-to-Unlock: Designing Productive Friction with GenAI

### Introduction
Generative AI (GenAI) holds significant potential to assist users in various tasks, such as writing. However, frictionless access to these powerful tools can lead to premature cognitive offloading, where users rely on the AI before fully engaging with their own ideas or the task itself. This can hinder learning and the development of user's own skills.

### Engage-to-Unlock Mechanism
To address this, the authors propose 'Engage-to-Unlock,' a design strategy that introduces 'productive friction.' This mechanism requires users to demonstrate meaningful engagement with the task before generative AI capabilities become available. The goal is to encourage initial human effort and idea generation, thereby optimizing the use of AI as a complementary tool rather than a primary substitute.

### Experimental Design
A controlled experiment was conducted with 398 participants to evaluate the effectiveness of Engage-to-Unlock. Participants were assigned to one of four conditions:
1.  **Human-Only:** No AI assistance.
2.  **Standard Chatbot:** Full, frictionless access to a chatbot.
3.  **Engage-to-Unlock:** AI capabilities unlocked after user engagement.
4.  **Time-Matched Unlock:** AI unlocked at the same time as Engage-to-Unlock, but independently of user engagement.

Participants completed a writing task and subsequently evaluated passages for evidence and inferential errors.

### Key Findings

*   **Effort Redistribution:** The Engage-to-Unlock condition led participants to spend more time on initial writing and less time on subsequent evaluation, compared to other AI-assisted conditions.
*   **No Increase in Overall Duration:** Despite the redistribution of effort, the total task duration did not increase.
*   **Increased AI Interaction:** Participants in the Engage-to-Unlock group submitted more prompts to the AI than in other AI-assisted conditions, suggesting sustained and active use.
*   **Efficient Evaluation:** The Engage-to-Unlock condition resulted in the highest accuracy-per-time efficiency during the evaluation phase.

### Conclusion
The findings suggest that introducing productive friction through mechanisms like Engage-to-Unlock can be beneficial. By encouraging early human engagement, GenAI systems can promote active user participation, maintain the user's cognitive involvement, and lead to more efficient downstream tasks, such as evaluating AI-generated content.
