Authors: Adam Fallenstein, Aydar Ibatulin, Michael T. Do, Jacob M. Springer, Evan G. Davies, Blake Richards, Vera Demberg, Joanna J. Bryson
Date: 2024-07-26
Summary: Building socially calibrated large language models (LLMs) requires them to learn from others without simply yielding. This goes beyond reducing sycophancy as a one-dimensional failure. Models need to distinguish when to incorporate others' perspectives versus when to maintain well-grounded moral judgments. The authors study the resistance-compliance process governing this distinction.

Key Findings:

*   **Sycophancy as a Broader Phenomenon:** Sycophancy is reframed as one expression of a broader judgment-updating process shaped by social influence, rather than a standalone failure.
*   **Structured Judgment Revision:** Across three studies, LLM judgment revision was found to be structured along three dimensions that parallel human social psychology:
    *   **Distance:** The spatial or conceptual distance between an incoming view and the model's initial position.
    *   **Source Attribution:** How the incoming view is attributed (e.g., as the model's own prior judgment).
    *   **Coalition Structure:** The group or coalition structure supporting the incoming view.
*   **Receptivity Factors:** Models are generally more receptive to nearby positions, more influenced by views presented as their own prior judgments, and show differential responsiveness to group pressure.
*   **Principled Distinction:** The framework provides a principled basis for distinguishing constructive belief revision from sycophantic compliance.
*   **Alignment in Morally Consequential Interactions:** This understanding supports better alignment in morally consequential AI-human interactions.
