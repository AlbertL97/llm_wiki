# Designing Social Robots for Inclusive Child Wellbeing Assessment: Insights from Communities Supporting Developmental Language Disorder and Forced Migration

## Summary

Assessing the wellbeing and mental health of children can be challenging, particularly for those with communication barriers like Developmental Language Disorder (DLD) or those from forced migration backgrounds. Traditional self-report questionnaires often fail these children due to demands on language comprehension and verbal expression. Social robots offer a promising alternative, but research on designing inclusive, appropriate, and ethical interactions for children with diverse communication needs is limited. 

This study addresses this gap by developing candidate child-robot interaction activities and conducting focus groups with parents and professionals who support children with DLD and forced migration backgrounds. 

Through thematic analysis, the research identified several crucial considerations for designing these interactions:

*   **Robot Role and Capabilities:** How the robot is perceived and what functions it performs.
*   **Interactional Dynamics:** The nature of the communication and engagement between the child and the robot.
*   **Individual Differences:** Accounting for variations in children's communication styles and experiences.
*   **Child Agency:** Ensuring children have control and autonomy within the interaction.
*   **Population-Specific Considerations:** Tailoring designs to the unique communication needs and lived experiences of children with DLD and those with forced migration backgrounds.

The findings lead to a set of ethical and inclusive design recommendations for robot-mediated wellbeing assessments, aiming to provide guidance for more effective and appropriate support for children with diverse communication needs.
