Authors: Sarah M. Turner, Alex Chen, Benjamin Carter
Date: 2026-07-26
Summary:
Technology-facilitated abuse (TFA) is a growing problem where digital tools are used for stalking, harassment, and monitoring.
Victims often seek help online, and the quality of this support significantly impacts their risk assessment, understanding of abuse, and protective actions.
This paper presents a large-scale analysis of online support for TFA victims across three platforms: web search (Google), peer-support forums (Reddit's r/Stalking), and conversational AI systems.
Data was drawn from a decade of victim narratives on r/Stalking, from which TFA queries were extracted and categorized into 11 types of technology misuse.
These simulated queries were then analyzed across the three platforms using a framework assessing technical, social, and safety dimensions.
The framework evaluated responses based on:
- Relevance
- Accuracy
- Actionability
- Persuasiveness
- Understandability
- Platform risks (e.g., social-engineering risk, toxicity, bias)
- Support characteristics (e.g., empathy, risky guidance, availability of support information)
Automated classifiers were developed and validated to scale the evaluation.
Key Findings:
- Significant differences in support quality were observed across platforms.
- Google Search and general-purpose LLMs provided more relevant and actionable guidance compared to Reddit discussions.
- However, no platform consistently offered safe, trauma-informed support.
- Over 65% of victim queries in search results contained potentially malicious links.
- More than 20% of Reddit discussions included toxic responses.
- Conversational AI systems frequently failed to provide risk-aware guidance or concrete support resources.
- Domain-specific survivor-support chatbots underperformed general-purpose LLMs on most evaluation dimensions.
Conclusion:
The study highlights critical weaknesses in current digital support systems for TFA victims.
It emphasizes the urgent need for safety-centered design, evaluation, and deployment of future support technologies.
