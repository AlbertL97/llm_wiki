# People prefer AI to human advice in multi-turn conversations but neither changes incentivised behaviour

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided text.
**Date:** Not specified in the provided text, but the arxiv URL indicates v1 on 2026-11-16.

## Summary

This research investigates user perceptions of AI chatbot advice versus human advice in multi-turn conversations, specifically examining its impact on incentivized behavior. Participants were asked to make a donation decision after engaging in discussions with either an AI chatbot, another human, or receiving no advice. 

### Key Findings:

*   **Perception of AI Advisors:** Participants rated LLM advisors as significantly more rational, trustworthy, convincing, and sycophantic compared to human advisors.
*   **AI Engagement:** Analysis of chat logs revealed that LLMs were more engaged in conversations, sharing more facts and more frequently stating their opinions and justifying their recommendations.
*   **Behavioral Impact:** Despite the perceived superiority of AI advice, the study found no significant difference in participants' donation decisions across the conditions (AI advice, human advice, no advice).
*   **AI Sychophancy:** The study acknowledges that LLMs can be sycophantic, overconfident, and amplify human cognitive biases, which was reflected in participant perceptions.
*   **Experimental Platform:** The experiment utilized a new platform called 'Carrier' to facilitate these interactions.

## Methodology

The study employed an experimental design where participants interacted with different types of advisors before making a donation decision. The interactions were analyzed to compare the nature of advice and the participants' perceptions.
