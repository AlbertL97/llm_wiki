Authors: J. Robert W. Thompson, Alex Buckland, Ben C. W. Wood, David J. Howard, Robert W. J. Williams, John M. C. Davis
Date: 2026-10-20
Title: Tactile In-Hand Object Rotation: A Comparative Study of Hand Morphology in Simulation and Hardware
Description: Continuous in-hand rotation requires coordinated contact maintenance and renewal, linking manipulation performance to both hand geometry and tactile feedback. We investigate these relationships through rotational progress, finger participation, and fingertip loading on two versions of the low-cost LEAP Hand with anthropomorphic and symmetric finger arrangements. Both hands are integrated with TacTip vision-based tactile sensors and use a shared reinforcement learning framework. Simulation evaluates full tactile, binary touch, and proprioception-only observations on 12 unseen objects; hardware experiments evaluate full tactile policies on ten objects under multiple hand orientations. In simulation, full tactile observations increase mean normalized rotation per episode over proprioception alone by 94.8% for the anthropomorphic hand and 66.9% for the symmetric hand, with longer mean episode durations and lower mean total fingertip loads. Hardware contact measurements reveal more differentiated finger participation in the anthropomorphic hand, while cumulative loading is more evenly distributed across the symmetric hand's fingers. Overall, the symmetric hand achieves a 32.0% higher mean rotation count and a 25.8% higher aggregate rotation rate, together with longer manipulation duration. Additional hardware force recordings show a 19.9% lower mean total fingertip load for this layout. These findings relate finger arrangement to contact organization and load sharing, and show that richer tactile information supports greater task progress at lower mean loading in both simulated embodiments.
Key Findings:
- Full tactile observations significantly improve in-hand object rotation compared to proprioception alone in both simulated anthropomorphic (94.8% increase) and symmetric (66.9% increase) hands.
- The anthropomorphic hand shows more differentiated finger participation, while the symmetric hand distributes load more evenly.
- The symmetric hand outperforms the anthropomorphic hand in mean rotation count (32.0% higher) and aggregate rotation rate (25.8% higher).
- The symmetric hand also achieves a lower mean total fingertip load (19.9% lower).
- Tactile feedback is crucial for effective manipulation, and hand morphology impacts contact organization and load sharing.
- Richer tactile information supports greater task progress with lower mean loading.
