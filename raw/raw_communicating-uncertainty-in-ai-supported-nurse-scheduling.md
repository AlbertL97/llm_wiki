# Communicating Uncertainty in AI-Supported Nurse Scheduling: Expert Co-Design and Evaluation of an Explainable AI Prototype

## Abstract
Nurse scheduling is a complex optimization problem in which nurses are assigned to shifts to meet staffing demands while complying with working-time limits, qualification requirements, and other operational and regulatory constraints. Artificial intelligence is increasingly being used to support this process by forecasting future staffing needs and providing advice on how to improve shift scheduling. Effective human-AI collaboration in nurse scheduling requires users to understand, interpret, and appropriately act on AI forecasts and advice. However, it remains insufficiently understood how healthcare professionals use transparency mechanisms like uncertainty communication and AI advice explanations when making staffing decisions.

## Methodology
Applying a human-centered design approach, this research iteratively developed a prototype decision support system for nurse scheduling. The prototype was evaluated through a mixed-methods user study with expert users. The study employed thematic analysis, questionnaire data, and eye tracking to examine how healthcare professionals interact with the system and how uncertainty communication and explanations of AI advice influence understanding, decision confidence, trust, and usability.

## Key Findings
*   Communicated uncertainty in AI forecasts was frequently misinterpreted.
*   Misinterpretation of uncertainty led to reduced decision confidence.
*   Explanations of AI advice were generally perceived positively.
*   Explanations did not significantly affect decision confidence or advice acceptance.

## Conclusion and Design Implications
The study concludes with eight design implications derived from the findings, offering insights for the design of more transparent and effective AI-supported decision support tools in healthcare. The findings highlight the challenges in effectively communicating uncertainty to users, even when explanations are provided.
