# Dimensions of Power: A Systematic Guide to Power Indices for Explainable AI

## Summary

The article addresses the understudied problem of selecting appropriate power indices for explainable AI (XAI). Power indices, originally from cooperative game theory, are adapted to quantify the influence of inputs on an AI system's output, thus facilitating explainability.

Key contributions and findings:

*   **Categorization of Power Indices:** The authors organize power indices along three attribution dimensions: single-player, set-based, and cardinality-based. This categorization provides a structured framework for understanding and applying these indices.
*   **Review and Generalization:** The paper reviews existing power indices within each dimension, generalizes them where applicable, and analyzes the formal principles they satisfy.
*   **Formal Properties and Proofs:** New proofs are provided for properties missing in the current literature.
*   **Cardinality-Based Setting:** It is shown that the cardinality-based setting can remove player-identity information while retaining some index-level distinctions, which can be relevant for certain explanation tasks.
*   **Practical Guidance:** The work offers concrete examples illustrating how the choice of dimension and index impacts attributions in practice, aiming to guide practitioners in selecting suitable power indices for specific application contexts.

While the direct focus is on XAI and game theory, the principles of attributing influence and understanding the 'power' of different components (inputs) can be indirectly related to human understanding of AI behavior, which is foundational for trust and interaction. Understanding how an AI's output is influenced is a step towards predicting and controlling its behavior, impacting user trust and the anthropomorphic perception of the AI's 'decision-making' process.
