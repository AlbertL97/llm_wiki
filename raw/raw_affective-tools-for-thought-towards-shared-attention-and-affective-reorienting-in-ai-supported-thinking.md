### Affective Tools for Thought: Towards Shared Attention and Affective Reorienting in AI-Supported Thinking

**Abstract:**
Current Tools for Thought (TfTs) predominantly view affect as either an impediment to cognitive progress or a signal to be optimized. This paper challenges this perspective, drawing on enactive cognitive science to argue that affect is fundamentally constitutive of cognition, actively reshaping the trajectory of thinking rather than merely influencing its speed.

**Core Barriers Identified:**

1.  **Lack of Shared Attention:** The absence of genuine 'caring' or directed attention towards the user's mode of engagement by the AI.
2.  **Lack of Affective Reorienting:** The AI's inability to leverage emotional moments to open new cognitive pathways, instead tending to reinforce predetermined ones.

**Proposed Design Strategies:**

1.  **Chain of Emotion X Chain of Thought:** A method that explicitly links emotional states with chains of reasoning, allowing affect to drive cognitive exploration.
2.  **Affective Mirror:** An AI mechanism that reflects and acknowledges the user's affective state, fostering a sense of understanding and shared experience.
3.  **Prompted Reorienting:** AI-initiated prompts designed to use emotional cues to guide the user towards alternative cognitive trajectories.

**Empirical Grounding:**
The proposed strategies are informed by empirical findings from a study involving a touch-aware conversational agent designed for embodied craft learning.

**Orientation:**
The work is presented as a set of provocations for future AI design, encouraging a more affectively integrated approach to AI-supported cognition.
