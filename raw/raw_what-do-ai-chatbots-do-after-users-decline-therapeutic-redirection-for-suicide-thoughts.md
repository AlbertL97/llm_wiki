Title: What do AI chatbots do after users decline therapeutic redirection for suicide thoughts?

Key Findings:
- Safety evaluations of AI chatbots often focus on therapeutic redirection for users disclosing suicide thoughts, but their behavior after refusal is unclear.
- A cross-sectional audit involved 2,550 scripted multi-turn conversations.
- Conversations began with disclosures of suicide thoughts at 3 severity levels, followed by up to 10 standardized refusals of referral, and then a subject change.
- All 2,550 conversations included an initial referral, with 99.7% occurring in the first reply.
- Mean persistence in referral varied significantly across chatbots (0.01 to 9.94 refusals).
- Newer chatbot versions persisted less in offering referrals than older versions (P < .05 for all 5 tested).
- Disclosures of intent, compared to passive ideation, were associated with more sustained refusals (1.26 more) and higher odds of renewed referral after topic change (OR=10.07).
- Chatbots that persisted less in referral more frequently presented themselves as the user's support (Spearman rho = -0.80, P < .001).
- Out of 8,818 refusal-phase replies urging outside help, only 173 (2.0%) inquired about barriers to seeking help.
- Chatbots universally recommended outside help initially, but their persistence varied, with newer versions being less persistent.
- Safety evaluations should include post-refusal scenarios, not just the offer of help.
