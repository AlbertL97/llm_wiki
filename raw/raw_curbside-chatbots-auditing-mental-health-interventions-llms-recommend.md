# Curbside Chatbots: Auditing the Mental Health Interventions Large Language Models Recommend to Clinicians, Caregivers, and Patients

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided text.
**Date:** 2026-11-10 (implied by the request's `Last updated` field and publication date format of the arXiv link)

**Summary:**

This research investigates the mental health advice provided by large language models (LLMs) when presented with clinical scenarios from the perspectives of clinicians, caregivers, and patients. The study audited 41 LLMs across 19 case vignettes and five questions, collecting 35,055 free-text responses. Key findings include:

*   **Differential Recommendations by Footing:** LLMs' recommendations for mental health interventions vary significantly depending on whether the query is framed as coming from a clinician describing a patient, a caregiver describing a loved one, or an individual describing themselves.
*   **Response Styles:** Clinicians often received a consultation-style response, while lay users (caregivers and patients) tended to receive hedged and simplified advice.
*   **Model Self-Rating Correlation:** Models largely recommended interventions that they themselves rated highly, suggesting a degree of internal consistency in their assessment, though this can be problematic when models recommend interventions they rate poorly.
*   **Conditional Recommendations:** When specifically asked about certain intervention types, models frequently suggested options that they themselves had rated below zero, often with explicit conditions attached.
*   **Living Audit Approach:** The study plans to release the instrument, code, and the full corpus as a "living audit" upon publication, enabling ongoing research and evaluation.

**Methodology:**

*   41 language models audited.
*   19 case vignettes used.
*   5 questions posed per vignette.
*   35,055 free-text responses collected.
*   Validated judges extracted named interventions.
*   29 models rated full option lists on a seven-point Likert scale.

**Implications:**

The findings raise concerns about the reliability and safety of LLM-generated mental health advice, particularly for non-expert users. The differing response styles highlight the potential for LLMs to adapt their communication but also underscore the need for careful evaluation of the accuracy and appropriateness of the advice given, especially in sensitive domains like mental health.
