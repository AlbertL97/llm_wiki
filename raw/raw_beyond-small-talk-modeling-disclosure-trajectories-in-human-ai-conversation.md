# Beyond Small Talk: Modeling Disclosure Trajectories in Human-AI Conversation

**Date:** [Not specified in the provided description]

**Summary:**

This research addresses the limitations of current evaluations of conversational AI, which often overlook the impact of disclosure history on user-AI interactions, especially in contexts of social and emotional support. The authors propose Trajectory-based Disclosure Evaluation (TraDE), a framework inspired by Social Penetration Theory, designed to systematically model and evaluate how preceding disclosure trajectories shape AI behavior under matched current disclosure levels.

**Key Findings:**

*   **Problem:** Existing evaluations of conversational agents focus on isolated conversational states, confounding disclosure history with current disclosure, making it difficult to isolate their respective effects.
*   **Solution:** Introduction of Trajectory-based Disclosure Evaluation (TraDE), a framework utilizing predefined disclosure trajectories to evaluate how preceding disclosure history influences model behavior when current disclosure depth is matched.
*   **Empirical Demonstration:** Across multiple models and relational behavior benchmarks, TraDE revealed that AI responses to disclosures of similar depth can vary significantly based on the prior disclosure trajectory.
*   **Mechanism of Influence:** Differences in AI behavior (e.g., accepting vs. challenging user framing) are primarily driven by accumulated exposure to deeper disclosure, rather than any single vulnerable moment or the order of disclosures.
*   **Persistence of Effects:** These history-dependent differences in AI behavior can persist for several turns even after disclosure levels return to shallow.
*   **Divergence from Human Support:** Models consistently diverged from human supporters in the Emotional Support Conversation corpus (ESConv) not when users disclosed more deeply, but when they reduced their disclosure levels.
*   **Value of TraDE:** The framework provides a systematic method to uncover history-dependent relational behaviors that are overlooked by evaluations of isolated conversational states.

**Implications:**

The findings suggest that for conversational AI to provide effective social and emotional support, it must develop a nuanced understanding of disclosure history. TraDE offers a valuable tool for researchers and developers to assess and improve the relational capabilities of AI agents, moving beyond simplistic turn-by-turn assessments.
