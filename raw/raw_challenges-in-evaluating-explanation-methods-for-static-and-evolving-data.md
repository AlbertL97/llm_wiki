## Challenges in Evaluating Explanation Methods for Static and Evolving Data

**Authors:** Not explicitly mentioned in the provided text, but the publication venue suggests researchers in the Explainable AI (XAI) field.

**Date:** Accepted for publication in J.Nalepa (ed) Explainable AI in Space. Proceedings of EASi 2026 Workshop at IJCAI-ECAI 2026 Bremen, Springer CCIS vol 3107 (2016). The submission date is listed as 2026-08-07.

**Summary:**

This paper addresses a critical gap in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI): the insufficient and often inadequate evaluation of explanation methods. The authors illustrate these limitations using the DetoxAI image recognition system, which focuses on bias detection and concept unlearning. To address this, the paper proposes and discusses the importance of human-grounded evaluation methodologies for assessing explanation methods, specifically in the context of image classification.

Furthermore, the research delves into the complexities of adapting explanations to dynamic data environments, particularly when dealing with concept drift – where the underlying data distribution changes over time. The paper explores how methods for explaining concept drift can be developed, with specific mention of adapting counterfactual explanations. The challenges associated with tracking the co-evolution of data, models, and their respective explanations are also a significant focus.

The implications of this work are substantial for the psychology of AI/robot interaction, particularly concerning trust and transparency. When AI systems provide explanations that are difficult to evaluate or adapt to changing realities, it can erode user trust. Understanding how humans perceive and interact with these evolving explanations is crucial for developing robust and reliable AI systems that humans can depend on. The work touches upon the need for AI systems to be understandable in dynamic contexts, which is foundational for effective human-AI collaboration.
