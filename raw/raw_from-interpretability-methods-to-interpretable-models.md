Authors: M. Adadi, M. Berrada, P. M. Grimaud, D. J. Newman, and A. M. Saidi
Date: 2026-09-03 (v1)

Summary:
The paper "From Interpretability Methods to Interpretable Models" addresses the current state of Explainable AI (XAI), particularly for computer vision. While a mature toolbox of interpretability methods (attribution, feature visualization, concept-based, circuit-based) exists, the authors argue that the field has predominantly focused on developing and comparing these methods rather than assessing their effectiveness in making models truly interpretable to humans. They propose a shift in focus from methods to models, advocating for two complementary approaches. First, utilizing existing tools to characterize and compare what different models represent and compute. Second, and more critically, establishing whether models can be understood by the independent human evaluators upon whom trust and certification depend, not just by experts who may already have domain knowledge. This human-centric understanding, they argue, can only be measured directly and cannot be inferred from method performance alone. The paper reviews the maturity of the XAI toolbox to support both approaches, surveys existing work on model comparison, draws parallels with systems neuroscience, and concludes with a model-centric agenda for XAI.
