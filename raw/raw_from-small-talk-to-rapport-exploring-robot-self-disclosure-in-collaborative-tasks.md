# From Small Talk to Rapport: Exploring Robot Self-Disclosure in Collaborative Tasks

**Authors:** [Not specified in the provided text]
**Date:** 2026-08-31 (assumed based on file version)

## Abstract:

People naturally engage in small talk and self-disclosure during collaboration to build rapport and social connections. As robots increasingly work alongside humans, understanding how to design these social behaviors effectively for engagement and teamwork is crucial. While prior research indicates that robot-initiated small talk can benefit human-robot collaboration, less is known about its optimal design. This work explores self-disclosure strategies within human-robot teams, specifically focusing on an industrial manipulator lacking anthropomorphic cues. 

## Methodology:

1. **Robot Development:** An LLM-driven manipulator was developed capable of small talk, implementing two disclosure strategies: low-disclosure and high-disclosure.
2. **User Study:** A study was conducted with 50 participants to evaluate the influence of robot self-disclosure in small talk on human-robot dynamics.

## Key Findings:

*   **Unexpected Disclosure Effect:** Contrary to expectations, participants disclosed more in the **low-disclosure** condition.
*   **Teaming and Coordination:** Participants in the **low-disclosure** condition reported stronger teaming and coordination compared to those in the high-disclosure condition.
*   **Influence of Prior Experience:** The positive effect of lower robot self-disclosure on teaming and coordination was more pronounced among users with previous experience collaborating with robots.
*   **Rapport and Connection:** The results suggest that increasing robot self-disclosure does not necessarily foster rapport, social connection, or reciprocal disclosure.
*   **Consideration of Other Factors:** Factors such as prior human-robot interaction (HRI) experience should be considered when designing robot social behaviors.

## Implications:

This research challenges the assumption that higher levels of robot self-disclosure directly lead to better human-robot relationships. It highlights the importance of considering user experience and potentially employing more nuanced, less disclosure-heavy strategies for effective human-robot teaming, especially in non-anthropomorphic robot contexts.
