Authors: Ethan A. D. Morrison, Nicholas Epley
Date: 2026-10-06

Title: Mind Perception Influences Perceived AI Companionability

Abstract:
Humans increasingly keep company with AI companions, yet whether mind perception (MP) precedes machine companionship remains untested. In a field-sampled experiment, participants received mind-attributive or mind-negating descriptions of an AIC before interacting with and rating its companionability. Baseline skepticism was high; minded primes somewhat reduced skepticism for connective coordination (CC; dyadic co-presence) but not eudaimonic exchange (EE; self-elevating links). Pre-interaction MP beliefs predicted companionability dimensionally: Agentic MP predicted EE potential while experiential MP predicted CC potential. Meaning in life moderated the agentic MP-companionability link, but exclusively for those already high in existential purpose. Results suggest two construal pathways to companionability--one through perceiving the AIC as an agent-resource and one through perceiving it as an attuning experiencer--both more accessible to humans already flourishing.

Key Findings:
- Mind perception (MP) plays a significant role in how humans perceive the companionability of AI companions (AIC).
- Priming participants with mind-attributive descriptions reduced skepticism for certain aspects of companionability (connective coordination) but not others (eudaimonic exchange).
- Pre-existing beliefs about an AI's agency predicted its potential for eudaimonic exchange, while beliefs about its experiential capacity predicted connective coordination.
- The relationship between perceived agency and companionability was moderated by an individual's sense of meaning in life, particularly for those with a high existential purpose.
- Two pathways to AI companionability are suggested: perceiving the AIC as an agent-resource or as an attuning experiencer.
- These pathways are more accessible to individuals who are already flourishing or have a strong sense of purpose.
