**Summary:**
This review paper explores the evolution of soft wearable robots beyond simple mechanical assistance towards integrated intelligent systems that deeply interact with and adapt to human users. The core concept introduced is Human-Centric Embodied Intelligence (HCEI), which posits that intelligence arises from the synergistic coupling of the human wearer and the robot. This intelligence is shaped by the robot's morphology, its sensing and actuation capabilities, and the wearer's own physiological and behavioral adaptations.

The paper introduces the Perception-Cognition-Actuation-Augmentation (PCAA) framework as a novel approach to designing these systems. This framework prioritizes perception and cognition, shifting the design paradigm from traditional actuator-centric development to one driven by understanding and adapting to human needs and states. The PCAA framework integrates advances in various fields, including soft materials, wearable sensing, artificial intelligence (AI), actuation technologies, human-robot interaction (HRI) principles, digital twins for simulation, clinical translation strategies, manufacturing processes, regulatory considerations, and ethical implications.

Key aspects highlighted:
*   **Human-Centricity:** The ultimate goal is to create wearable robots that are personalized, predictive, and seamlessly integrated into users' lives.
*   **Embodied Intelligence:** Intelligence is not solely within the robot but emerges from the dynamic interaction within the human-robot system.
*   **PCAA Framework:** A structured approach that emphasizes understanding human perception and cognition to drive the design of wearable robotic systems.
*   **Interdisciplinary Approach:** The review synthesizes advancements across materials science, AI, robotics, HCI, medicine, and ethics.
*   **Long-term Benefits:** Focus on developing systems that users can wear comfortably, trust, and derive sustained benefits from, particularly in areas like rehabilitation, occupational assistance, and human augmentation.

The paper aims to provide a unified perspective to guide future research and foster collaboration for the effective translation of next-generation soft wearable robots.
