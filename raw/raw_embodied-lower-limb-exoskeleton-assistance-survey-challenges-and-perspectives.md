# Embodied Lower-limb Exoskeleton Assistance: Survey, Challenges, and Perspectives

## Introduction
This paper reviews embodied lower-limb exoskeletons, moving beyond traditional AI algorithm or mechanical design-focused reviews. It defines embodied lower-limb exoskeletons and structures the literature around four key dimensions: structural self-adaptation, task adaptation, user-specific adaptation, and control self-regulation.

## Key Dimensions and Challenges

*   **Structural Self-Adaptation:** Focuses on lightweight and compliant design for better integration with the human body.
*   **Task Adaptation:** Deals with the AI's ability to adapt exoskeleton assistance to complex and diverse tasks.
*   **User-Specific Adaptation:** Addresses the need for exoskeletons to tailor their assistance to individual users' needs and characteristics.
*   **Control Self-Regulation:** Involves robust, compliant control strategies that ensure safety and smooth interaction.

Key challenges identified across these dimensions include data acquisition and fusion, optimization of complex policies, and ensuring robust and compliant control.

## Human-Robot Interaction Framework
The authors propose an embodied perspective that emphasizes the closed-loop relationship among mechanical structure, sensing, and control. This framework is presented as crucial for analyzing exoskeleton performance and advancing research in human-robot interaction.

## Future Directions
The review identifies limitations in current research and outlines future directions focused on developing lightweight, comfortable, and practical physical assistance systems. The ultimate goal is to achieve more seamless and effective integration of exoskeletons with human users.
