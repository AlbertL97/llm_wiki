## Abstract
To effectively integrate AI into high-stakes, critical environments such as healthcare, autonomous driving, and aviation--and to advance toward higher levels of automation and seamless human-AI collaboration--building trust in AI-driven solutions is essential. Trust, in turn, is closely linked to the explainability of AI systems. The rapid advancements in AI across various domains have underscored the challenges of establishing trust, raising increasing interest in AI explainability even more when applied to deep learning. In this context, the present work aims to explore the application of explainability techniques to Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, specifically within the safety-critical domain of Air Traffic Control (ATC).

## Methodology
Using a simplified ATC environment as an initial testbed, an intelligent agent is trained with a reinforcement learning algorithm to make decisions on alternative flight routes that avoid no-fly zones. As a preliminary explainability approach, a saliency map is employed, providing insights into the input features that most significantly influence the agent's decision-making process.

## Key Findings/Contributions

*   **Importance of Trust in Critical AI Applications:** The paper highlights the critical need for trust in AI systems deployed in high-stakes environments like Air Traffic Control (ATC) to enable seamless human-AI collaboration.
*   **Link between Trust and Explainability:** Trust is established as being closely linked to the explainability of AI systems, particularly for advanced techniques like deep learning.
*   **Application of Explainable RL to ATC:** The research explores the application of explainability techniques to Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms specifically for ATC decision support.
*   **Saliency Maps for RL Explainability:** A preliminary approach using saliency maps is proposed to provide insights into the input features influencing an RL agent's decisions in a simplified ATC scenario.
*   **Reinforcement Learning for Route Planning:** An RL agent was trained to make flight route decisions to avoid no-fly zones in a simulated ATC environment.

## Future Work/Implications
This work lays the groundwork for developing more transparent and trustworthy AI systems in critical domains, potentially leading to safer and more efficient operations through better human-AI interaction.
