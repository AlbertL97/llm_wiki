# Delegating or Doing? Understanding User Behavior in Hybrid Human-Agent Interfaces

**Authors:** [Unnamed Authors]
**Date:** 2026-08-22

## Summary

Large Language Models (LLMs) are being integrated into applications, offering users the choice to interact directly or delegate tasks to conversational agents. This research investigates how users balance these modalities in a hybrid interface, specifically a content management system augmented with an LLM agent. A study (N=73) compared three interaction modes: Traditional-Only, AI-First, and Hybrid. Key findings indicate that AI assistance significantly reduces interaction effort (fewer clicks, navigations, scrolling) but does not notably decrease task completion time. The study also found that the decision to delegate is largely idiosyncratic, driven by individual differences rather than the type of task or perceived risk. Approximately half of the variance in assistant use was attributable to these individual factors.

## Key Findings

*   **Reduced Interaction Effort:** AI-assisted interaction led to significant reductions in clicks, page navigations, and scrolling, indicating a lower interaction effort for users.
*   **No Significant Time Savings:** Despite reduced effort, task completion time did not differ significantly across interaction modes.
*   **Delegation is User-Centric:** The propensity to delegate actions to the AI agent varied substantially between participants rather than between specific CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) operation types. Individual differences accounted for approximately 50% of the variance in assistant use (ICC = .50).
*   **Risk Not a Major Factor in Delegation:** Users did not appear to systematically avoid delegating higher-risk actions.
*   **Primary Benefit of AI:** The main advantage of human-agent interfaces may lie in minimizing interaction effort, not necessarily in speeding up task completion.

## Methodology

*   **Study Design:** Between-subjects study.
*   **Participants:** N=73.
*   **Interface:** Web-based content management system augmented with an LLM agent (using Model Context Protocol - MCP).
*   **Interaction Modes:** Traditional-Only, AI-First, Hybrid.
*   **Task Scenarios:** Sixteen CRUD tasks.
*   **Data Collected:** Task completion time, interaction logs, delegation behavior.

## Implications

The findings suggest that the design of human-agent interfaces should consider the significant impact of individual user characteristics on delegation behavior. The benefit of AI assistance may be best leveraged by focusing on optimizing the user experience through reduced cognitive load and interaction effort.
