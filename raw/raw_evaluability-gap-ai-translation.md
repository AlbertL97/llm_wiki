Authors: Michael L. Bernstein, Jiarui Lu, Qian Zhang, Justin Z. Wu
Date: 2026-08-19

Summary:
This paper investigates the 'evaluability gap' in AI translation, where readable AI output can mask deficiencies in preserving the original source content, even when the source is presented. The research examines how the conditions of the source text and the rendering of the output affect perceived translation quality, and subsequently, how these perceptions relate to trust in the AI and the user's willingness to share personal information.

Key Findings:

*   **Readability vs. Fidelity:** Readable AI outputs can create an 'evaluability gap,' where users' overall quality judgments do not accurately reflect the translation's fidelity to the source, even when the source is visible.
*   **Impact of Source Text and Output Rendering:** A 2x2 experiment (N=306) using TransLingo examined simple narratives and complex literary-philosophical prose. It compared AI-generated readability-oriented outputs with researcher-revised fidelity-oriented outputs.
    *   For simple narratives, participants rated fidelity-oriented outputs higher than readability-oriented outputs.
    *   For complex prose, no significant difference in perceived quality was found between rendering types, indicating that source access did not guarantee accurate evaluation of content retention.
*   **Influence on Trust and Anthropomorphism:** The study found a pattern where perceived intelligence, agency-oriented anthropomorphic attribution, and task-performance trust were influenced by the source text condition, mirroring the differences in perceived quality.
*   **Appraisal Structure and Disclosure Willingness:** A structural equation model (SEM) revealed concurrent associations between perceived quality, perceived intelligence, anthropomorphic attribution, task-performance trust, and stated disclosure willingness. Task-performance trust was the most proximal correlate of willingness to disclose personal text.
*   **Distinction between Source Access and Evaluability:** The findings distinguish between simply providing access to the source text and enabling users to effectively evaluate the output's accuracy based on that source, especially for challenging content.
*   **Implications for Data Disclosure:** The study separates the support for evaluating translation output from the support for making data-handling decisions about entrusting personal text to an AI system.
