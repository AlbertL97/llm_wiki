# Just Between Us? Exploring a Wearable-Mediated Channel for Discreet Human-Agent Interaction in a Shared Game Setting

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided abstract.
**Date:** 2026-11-04 (based on arXiv version date)

## Key Findings:

*   **Problem:** In shared environments, social robots may need to communicate privately with an individual without others easily interpreting the exchange.
*   **Proposed Solution:** A wearable-mediated channel using in-ear audio messages and deliberate head tilts (rightward) for discreet interaction.
*   **Experiment Setup:** 40 participants (20 pairs) played a card game with a screen-based conversational agent (controlled via Wizard-of-Oz). One participant (wearer) used the discreet channel, while the other (non-wearer) was informed afterward.
*   **Task:** The channel conveyed information to help the wearer or agent character select a defeatable opponent.
*   **Feasibility:** The information exchange and opponent selection occurred as intended in 39 out of 40 turns, demonstrating the channel's effectiveness.
*   **Wearer Experience:** Wearers reported low perceived pressure/tension and favorable usability immediately after use, with no significant difference between conditions (with or without the channel).
*   **Non-Wearer Experience:** Retrospective interviews revealed varied non-wearer awareness of the interaction and contrasting perceptions regarding teamwork and fairness.
*   **Implications:** The findings support the feasibility of wearable-mediated discreet communication for human-agent interaction and highlight awareness and fairness as crucial design considerations for future systems.
