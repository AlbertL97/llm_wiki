# Trusting AI in Competitive Markets

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided text.

**Date:** 2026-08-26 (v1)

**Summary:**

This research explores the divergence in trust people place in AI advice, particularly in competitive market scenarios where AI recommendations cannot be definitively validated by their own success but rather by competitors' responses.

**Problem Definition:**

*   Individuals' trust in AI advice is not static; it can deepen for some users while eroding for others over time.
*   The study focuses on oligopoly pricing, a context where AI advice's effectiveness is contingent on rivals' reactions.

**Methodology/Results:**

*   A laboratory experiment was conducted with 273 sellers competing across 91 three-seller markets over 30 rounds.
*   The experiment manipulated the presence of AI pricing recommendations and the gender composition of the market (female-only, male-only, or mixed).
*   **Key Finding 1:** The gender composition of the market significantly shapes how sellers learn from AI recommendations and influences pricing outcomes.
*   **Key Finding 2:** In female-only markets, AI recommendations led to a 29% increase in prices and a 39% increase in profits.
*   **Key Finding 3:** In male-only and mixed-gender markets, AI recommendations had no significant effect on prices or profits.
*   **Key Finding 4:** A Non-Homogeneous Hidden Markov Model revealed distinct dynamic associations based on market composition:
    *   In female-only markets, profitable rounds predicted increasing adherence to the AI, consistent with learned trust and self-serving attribution.
    *   In male-only and mixed-gender markets, the pattern reversed, indicating declining adherence after profitable rounds.
*   This observed pattern contradicts some previous evidence regarding gender and AI interactions.

**Managerial Implications:**

*   Platform governance and regulatory oversight should consider not only the AI algorithm itself but also the human factors influencing its adoption and effectiveness.
*   Understanding the socio-demographic context, such as gender composition, is crucial for predicting and managing AI's impact.
