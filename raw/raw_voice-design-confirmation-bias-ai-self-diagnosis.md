Title: What's the Difference? Voice Design and Confirmation Bias in AI-Assisted Self-Diagnosis: Anthropomorphic vs. Machine-Like Voice Output
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21379v1

Key Findings:
- AI symptom checkers are increasingly used for health information, raising concerns about their influence on user decisions, particularly confirmation bias.
- The study examined whether the voice design of an AI symptom checker (anthropomorphic vs. machine-like) modulates confirmation bias.
- A within-subjects online experiment with 81 participants and 648 trials was conducted.
- Participants made an initial diagnosis and rated confidence, then heard an AI recommendation (confirming or contradicting) delivered in either an anthropomorphic or machine-like voice, before making a final decision.
- A strong, direction-dependent influence of the AI recommendation was observed: participants revised their diagnosis in 45.7% of trials when the AI contradicted them, compared to only 1.2% when it confirmed them.
- User confidence increased when the AI confirmed their diagnosis but showed little change when it contradicted them.
- Decision-making time increased after receiving a contradictory AI recommendation.
- Crucially, the study found no significant difference in these effects between the anthropomorphic and machine-like voices, nor any interaction effect between voice type and recommendation direction.
- A manipulation check confirmed that participants perceived the voices as markedly different.
- The findings suggest that the content and direction of the AI's recommendation are the primary drivers of confirmation bias in AI-assisted self-diagnosis, rather than the anthropomorphism of its voice.
