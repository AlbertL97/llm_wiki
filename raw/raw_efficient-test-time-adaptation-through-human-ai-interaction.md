# Efficient Test-Time Adaptation through Human-AI Interaction

## Key Findings and Summary

This research addresses the gap between AI agents trained on general data and the specific, often unarticulated, needs of individual users, particularly professionals. The core problem identified is that AI-generated outputs rarely meet the high personal standards required for professional tasks, where individual expertise lies in deviating from the average.

The proposed solution is Test-Time Adaptation through Human-AI Interaction (TAHI). This method leverages iterative human-agent interactions to capture implicit user criteria that are difficult to specify upfront but are consistently applied. TAHI integrates this interaction data into the agent's context and weights, and employs an evolving rubric module to crystallize each user's unique training and evaluation criteria.

**Experimental Results:**

*   **Adaptation:** TAHI was tested on 30 individuals across writing and visual creation domains, involving 600 tasks. 
*   **Performance Improvement:** Adapted agents showed significant improvements in solo task success, ranging from 4.5% to 20.9%, achieved with only tens of tasks for adaptation.
*   **Scalable Annotation Tool:** The evolving rubric module proved effective as a scalable annotation tool, identifying 16.0% to 22.3% more failures than rubrics generated by LMs or humans alone.
*   **Generalization:** While agents were personalized, their improvements in task success (up to 8.8%) also generalized across users.

**Conclusion:**

TAHI effectively closes the gap between general AI capabilities and individual professional expertise by utilizing cross-session interaction data. It not only enhances agent performance for individual users but also offers a practical method for developing robust evaluation criteria, demonstrating the power of continuous human-AI collaboration.
