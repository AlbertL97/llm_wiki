# When Should I Trust This? Designing and Evaluating Factuality Indicators for LLM-based Chatbots

**Authors:** K. T. H. Ho, T. D. R. Lee, J. G. Chen, S. G. Wu

**Date:** 2026-10-23 (Original Submission)

**Abstract:**
Generative AI systems increasingly mediate how people seek information, yet communicating AI factuality to laypeople remains challenging. Through a mixed-methods investigation involving interviews (N=8), a survey (N=1,000), and an experiment (N=5,151), we examine how people evaluate AI-generated information for gardening, mathematical, and medical queries, and how factuality indicators (confidence and sources) shape perceptions. Our interviews and survey show people selectively verify AI responses using external and AI-provided evidence, moderated by contextual factors. Although confidence-indicators affected trust and accuracy perceptions, people hold diverse, ambiguous interpretations of AI confidence. Sources were viewed as crucial, yet users raised concerns about source reliability and verification effort. Our experiment showed that confidence and source-based indicators influenced initial perceptions, but effects disappeared once participants learned the response's correctness. Medical queries elicited skepticism regardless of indicator. Findings motivate helping users interpret model confidence and integrating source information into the interface to support calibrated trust in AI-supported information-seeking.

**Key Findings:**

*   **Information Seeking Behavior:** People selectively verify AI responses, using both external and AI-provided evidence, influenced by contextual factors.
*   **Factuality Indicators:**
    *   **Confidence:** Confidence indicators influenced perceptions of trust and accuracy, but users interpreted AI confidence in diverse and ambiguous ways.
    *   **Sources:** Source information was considered crucial, though users expressed concerns about the reliability of sources and the effort required for verification.
*   **Experimental Results:** Confidence and source indicators impacted initial perceptions but lost significance once participants knew the correctness of the AI response.
*   **Domain Specificity:** Medical queries consistently elicited skepticism from users, irrespective of the factuality indicators presented.
*   **Design Implications:** There is a need to improve user understanding of model confidence and integrate source information directly into AI interfaces to foster calibrated trust.

**Methodology:**

*   Mixed-methods approach.
*   Interviews (N=8): Qualitative exploration of user evaluation strategies.
*   Survey (N=1,000): Broad examination of user perceptions and verification habits.
*   Experiment (N=5,151): Quantitative assessment of the impact of factuality indicators on trust and accuracy perceptions across different query domains (gardening, mathematical, medical).
