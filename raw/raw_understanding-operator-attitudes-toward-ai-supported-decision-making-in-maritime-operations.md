Title: Understanding Operator Attitudes Toward AI-Supported Decision Making in Maritime Operations

Summary:
Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS) and AI-supported decision assistants are poised to revolutionize maritime operations. However, their successful and safe integration hinges on the perceptions and trust of maritime professionals. This paper details a survey study conducted among maritime stakeholders to gauge their attitudes towards an AI-supported assistant designed for collision-avoidance scenarios.

Methodology:
Participants completed established and adapted questionnaires to assess technology anxiety, trust in automation, and the quality of explanations provided by the AI. Sentiment and thematic analysis were applied to open-ended responses to gain deeper qualitative insights.

Key Findings:
- A generally positive disposition towards maritime technology was observed.
- No significant age-related differences in openness to AI support were identified.
- Trust in automation remained stable across different operational scenarios.
- Ratings for explanation quality were sensitive to specific scenarios and demonstrated multidimensionality.
- Open-ended responses revealed that participants valued the AI's support for decision-making, enhancement of situation awareness, and building confidence.
- Concerns were raised regarding AI reliability, the potential for over-reliance on AI systems, and the consequent loss of human expertise.

Recommendations:
- Future maritime AI systems should prioritize supporting calibrated reliance rather than solely aiming to boost automation or trust.
- Design should focus on transparency, reliability, and operational relevance.
- Continuous involvement of domain experts in the design and implementation process is crucial.
