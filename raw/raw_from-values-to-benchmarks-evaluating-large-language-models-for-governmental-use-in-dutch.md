# From Values to Benchmarks: Evaluating Large Language Models for Governmental Use in Dutch

**Authors:** Not specified in the provided abstract.
**Date:** 2026-08-11 (based on the URL `2608.09925v1` and the requested last updated date).

## Comprehensive Summary:

This research addresses the challenge of evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) for deployment in governmental contexts, particularly in non-English speaking regions like the Netherlands. The authors developed a framework called "Grip on LLMs" in collaboration with experts from a Dutch municipal organization. This framework aims to bridge the gap between the values of public administration and the linguistic requirements of specific contexts.

The evaluation process involved an advisory board, user research, and a survey of users of a civil-servant chatbot. Through these methods, six key evaluation dimensions were identified:

1.  **Factuality:** The accuracy of the LLM's responses.
2.  **Honesty:** The LLM's ability to acknowledge its limitations and uncertainties.
3.  **Social Bias:** The presence of unfair or prejudiced outputs.
4.  **Energy Consumption:** The environmental impact of running the LLM.
5.  **Cost:** The financial expenses associated with LLM usage.
6.  **Training Data Transparency:** Information about the data used to train the LLM.

These dimensions were operationalized into a benchmark suite that tested over 30 multilingual and Dutch-specific LLMs. The findings indicate that no single LLM is superior across all dimensions, and significant trade-offs exist. Specifically, higher quality LLMs tend to have a greater environmental impact and financial cost. Bias was found to be largely independent of these factors.

A notable finding is the distinction between factuality and honesty. High factuality does not imply high honesty, meaning an LLM might provide correct answers but still fail to admit when it doesn't know something. This has critical implications for user trust in AI systems.

To make the evaluation results accessible and actionable for a diverse range of stakeholders (from engineers to policymakers), a user-friendly, publicly available model overview was developed. This emphasizes the importance of informed decision-making in the selection and deployment of LLMs in public services.
