# Benchmarking the Benchmarks: Evaluating Benchmarks for Conversational Agents

**Summary:**
Task-oriented conversational agents are commonly evaluated using curated or automatically generated benchmarks. However, the quality of these benchmarks themselves is often not rigorously assessed. This lack of quality control can lead to unreliable evaluations due to issues such as inconsistent tasks, overly simplistic scenarios, or limited policy coverage within the benchmarks.

To address this, the paper proposes a reference-free framework that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to act as judges. These LLM judges are used to assess key aspects of benchmark quality, including:

*   **Consistency:** Whether the tasks and scenarios within the benchmark are logically coherent.
*   **Complexity:** The difficulty and nuance of the scenarios presented.
*   **Policy Coverage:** The extent to which the benchmark adequately represents the range of situations an agent might encounter.

The framework also aims to provide actionable diagnostics, highlighting specific weaknesses in the benchmarks.

The authors validate their framework through:

*   Demonstrating agreement with independent human annotations, suggesting the LLM judges can effectively mirror human judgment.
*   Evaluating benchmarks generated by LLMs with varying capabilities, showcasing the framework's ability to differentiate based on LLM proficiency.
*   Assessing benchmarks subjected to controlled quality-degrading perturbations, further proving the metrics' sensitivity to benchmark quality.

Across different domains and judge models, the proposed metrics consistently distinguish between benchmarks of varying quality levels. The framework's applicability is further extended to manually curated benchmarks, making it a versatile tool for both synthetic and human-created evaluation sets.

**Key Findings:**
*   Current benchmarks for conversational agents often lack rigorous quality assessment, leading to unreliable evaluations.
*   A new framework using LLM judges can effectively evaluate benchmark consistency, complexity, and policy coverage without needing reference answers.
*   The LLM-based evaluation framework shows agreement with human annotations and can differentiate between benchmark quality levels.
*   The framework is applicable to both automatically generated and manually curated benchmarks.
