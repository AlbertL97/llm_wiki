# Seeing through the Eyes of AI: Situated Explainability in Augmented Reality

**Authors:** Anya V. Petrova, Benjamin R. Smith, Chen Zhang, Sarah K. Lee
**Date:** 2026-10-03 (v1)

## Summary

This research addresses the challenge of understanding AI decisions, particularly when AI operates within physical environments. Traditional Explainable AI (XAI) methods often present explanations on 2D screens, requiring users to mentally map this information back to the real world, which can be cognitively taxing and lead to misinterpretations. The authors propose "Situated Explainability" as a solution, leveraging Augmented Reality (AR) to display AI explanations directly in the user's physical workspace in real time.

Key aspects of the proposal and findings:

*   **Problem Identified:** Existing XAI methods create a disconnect between abstract explanations and the physical context of AI-driven actions. This separation hinders effective interpretation and trust.
*   **Proposed Solution:** Integrate XAI into AR environments. This allows explanations to be spatially mapped and presented directly alongside the objects or locations relevant to the AI's decision.
*   **Methodology:** The paper explores how established XAI techniques can be adapted for AR interfaces. It also includes user studies to gather insights into the practical experience of using AR for situated explainability.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Enhanced contextual understanding of AI behavior.
    *   Reduced cognitive load for users.
    *   Potentially improved trust and adoption of AI systems operating in physical spaces.
*   **User Experience:** Preliminary insights suggest that AR-based explanations can improve comprehension, although design considerations for effective AR overlays are crucial.

This work positions AR as a powerful tool for bridging the gap between AI intelligence and human comprehension in interactive, real-world scenarios.
