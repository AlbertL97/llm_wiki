## Key Findings:

*   **Fear as a significant emotion:** The review highlights that fear is a prevalent and important emotion experienced by older adults when interacting with social robots.
*   **Sources of fear:** Identified sources of fear include:
    *   Apprehension about the robot's capabilities and potential for malfunctions.
    *   Concerns regarding physical harm or damage caused by the robot.
    *   Anxiety related to the robot's autonomy and unpredictable behavior.
    *   Influence of negative media portrayals and science fiction narratives about robots.
    *   Uncertainty about data privacy and security.
*   **Impact on interaction:** Fear can significantly impede the adoption and acceptance of social robots by older adults, hindering potential benefits in areas like companionship and care.
*   **Mitigation strategies and design considerations:** The review suggests that understanding these fears is crucial for designing robots and interaction protocols that promote comfort, safety, and trust.
*   **Gaps in research:** There is a need for more empirical research to systematically investigate the nature, prevalence, and underlying causes of fear in this demographic, as well as effective strategies for its reduction.
*   **Focus on older adults:** The review specifically addresses the unique perspectives and vulnerabilities of older adults, a demographic often targeted for social robot applications.
