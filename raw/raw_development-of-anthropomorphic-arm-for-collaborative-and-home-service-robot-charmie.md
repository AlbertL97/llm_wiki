## Abstract:
Service Robots are becoming a reality and are progressing by leaps and bounds. Alongside this avenue of robotics is also the field of prosthetics, which is evolving rapidly. These topics are hot cakes in the industry and are widely developed. Some have one quality, and some have others, but are making a shoreline towards success. The development of arm and hand as individual or as a package is becoming common as more and more advanced collaborative robots come to the surface and join their human counterparts in the real world. This dissertation presents an open-source 3D-printed arm which was assembled and programmed on two fronts.

## Key Findings and Details:

*   **Focus:** Development of an anthropomorphic arm for collaborative and home service robots.
*   **Motivation:** Rapid advancements in service robotics and prosthetics, and the increasing integration of robots with humans.
*   **Project Deliverable:** An open-source, 3D-printed anthropomorphic arm.
*   **Assembly and Programming:** The arm was assembled and programmed with two primary control methods:
    *   **Teleoperation with ML-based Hand Pose Estimation:** Utilizing a camera and machine learning to estimate hand pose for control.
    *   **Kinematics using Denavit-Hartenberg Method:** Defining motion and determining the end-effector's position in 3D space.
*   **Application Domain:** Collaborative and home service robots, suggesting direct human-robot interaction and potential for task assistance in domestic environments.
*   **Significance:** Contributes to the growing field of advanced robotic manipulation designed to work alongside humans.
